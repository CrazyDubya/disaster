## Water Requirements by Timeline

### 72 Hours (3 Days)
**Minimum:** 3 gallons per person
**Recommended:** 4-5 gallons per person
- 1 gallon/day drinking and food prep
- Additional for hygiene

**Storage:** Bottled water or home storage containers
**Purification:** Backup method (tablets or filter)

### 2 Weeks
**Minimum:** 14 gallons per person
**Recommended:** 20-25 gallons per person
- Includes basic hygiene and sanitation
- Some cooking water

**Storage:** Mix of bottled and bulk storage
**Purification:** Primary purification method required

### 1 Month
**Minimum:** 30 gallons per person
**Recommended:** 40-50 gallons per person
- Full hygiene and sanitation
- Cooking
- Light cleaning

**Storage:** Bulk storage containers (55-gallon drums)
**Purification:** Reliable purification system
**Rotation:** Regular rotation schedule

### 3-6 Months
**Minimum:** 90-180 gallons per person
**Recommended:** Renewable source + storage
- All household uses
- Limited laundry
- Dish washing

**Storage:** Multiple 55-gallon drums or water tanks
**Purification:** Multi-stage filtration
**Source:** Rainwater collection or well access

### 1 Year+
**Requirement:** Sustainable water source
- 365+ gallons per person stored, OR
- Renewable source (well, spring, rain catchment)

**Storage:** Large tanks (500-1,000+ gallons)
**Purification:** Comprehensive filtration and treatment
**Source:** Well, spring, rainwater harvesting system

## Water Storage Methods

### Short-Term Storage (2 Weeks - 3 Months)

#### Commercial Bottled Water
**Pros:**
- No preparation needed
- Known quantity
- Portable
- Safe if sealed

**Cons:**
- Expensive for large quantities
- Takes up space
- Plastic waste
- Can degrade over time

**Shelf Life:** Check printed expiration date (typically 1-2 years)
**Rotation:** Every 6-12 months
**Storage:** Cool, dark place away from chemicals/solvents

#### Food-Grade Containers

**2-Liter Soda Bottles (FREE!)**
1. Rinse thoroughly with water
2. Sanitize with 1 tsp bleach per quart of water
3. Rinse with clean water
4. Fill with tap water
5. Add 2 drops bleach per liter (if not chlorinated)
6. Cap tightly
7. Label with date

**Shelf Life:** 6 months (rotate regularly)
**Cost:** Free
**Capacity:** 2 liters (0.5 gallon)

**5-Gallon Water Jugs**
- Camping/office water cooler jugs
- Food-grade plastic only
- Easy to transport
- Stackable

**Shelf Life:** 1 year
**Cost:** $8-15 empty, $6-10 refilled
**Capacity:** 5 gallons (19 liters)

**Water Bricks/WaterBobs**
- Stackable, space-efficient
- Durable
- Multi-purpose containers

**Shelf Life:** Several years
**Cost:** $15-35 each
**Capacity:** 3.5 gallons per brick

### Medium-Term Storage (3-12 Months)

#### 55-Gallon Drums
**Best for:** Basements, garages, long-term storage

**Setup:**
1. Purchase food-grade barrel ($50-100)
2. Clean thoroughly
3. Add water preserver treatment (optional)
4. Fill with clean water
5. Seal tightly
6. Label with date

**Shelf Life:** 5 years with preservative, 6-12 months without
**Rotation:** Annually or when treating
**Space:** 55 gallons = 2 ft diameter, 3 ft height
**Weight:** 440+ lbs when full (plan floor strength!)

**Important:** Once full, almost impossible to move

#### Water Storage Tanks (100-500 Gallons)
- Polyethylene tanks
- Can be indoor or outdoor
- May require stand or platform
- Various shapes (upright, horizontal)

**Shelf Life:** Indefinite with proper treatment
**Cost:** $100-500 depending on size
**Maintenance:** Clean annually, add preservative

### Long-Term Storage (1+ Years)

#### Large Cisterns (500-10,000+ Gallons)
- Underground or above-ground
- Concrete, fiberglass, or polyethylene
- Requires significant space
- May require permits

**Best for:** Rural properties, homesteads, multi-family
**Cost:** $500-5,000+
**Maintenance:** Professional cleaning every 3-5 years

#### Rainwater Harvesting Systems
- Collection from roof
- Gutter system to tank
- Filtration and treatment
- Can provide ongoing water supply

**Capacity:** Depends on roof size and rainfall
**Formula:** 1 inch of rain on 1,000 sq ft roof = ~600 gallons
**Cost:** $1,000-5,000+ for complete system
**Legality:** Check local laws (restricted in some areas)

## Water Purification Techniques

### Understanding Water Contaminants

**Biological:**
- Bacteria (E. coli, Salmonella)
- Viruses (Hepatitis A, Norovirus)
- Protozoa (Giardia, Cryptosporidium)

**Chemical:**
- Heavy metals
- Pesticides
- Industrial contaminants

**Physical:**
- Sediment
- Debris
- Turbidity (cloudiness)

**Different methods remove different contaminants!**

### Boiling (Most Reliable for Biological)

**Method:**
1. Filter or let sediment settle if water is cloudy
2. Bring water to rolling boil
3. Boil for 1 minute (3 minutes above 6,500 ft elevation)
4. Let cool before drinking

**Effectiveness:**
- ✅ Kills all bacteria
- ✅ Kills all viruses
- ✅ Kills all protozoa
- ❌ Does NOT remove chemicals
- ❌ Does NOT remove heavy metals
- ❌ Does NOT remove sediment (pre-filter)

**Cost:** Fuel only
**Shelf Life:** N/A (method, not product)

**Free Resource:**
**CDC - Making Water Safe**
- **URL:** https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/index.html
- **Content:** Official boiling guidelines

### Chemical Treatment

#### Household Bleach

**Unscented, 5-9% sodium hypochlorite only**

**Method:**
1. Filter or settle cloudy water first
2. Add bleach:
   - Clear water: 8 drops (1/8 tsp) per gallon
   - Cloudy water: 16 drops (1/4 tsp) per gallon
3. Mix well
4. Let stand 30 minutes
5. Should have slight chlorine odor (if not, repeat and wait 15 min)

**Effectiveness:**
- ✅ Kills most bacteria
- ✅ Kills most viruses
- ⚠️ NOT effective against Cryptosporidium
- ❌ Does NOT remove chemicals
- ❌ Does NOT remove sediment

**Cost:** ~$2-4/gallon (treats thousands of gallons)
**Shelf Life:** Bleach degrades 50% per year (replace annually)

**Free Resource:**
**FEMA - How to Make Water Safe**
- **URL:** https://www.fema.gov/print/pdf/node/318019
- **Content:** Bleach purification instructions

#### Purification Tablets

**Types:**
- Iodine tablets
- Chlorine dioxide tablets
- Sodium dichloroisocyanurate (NaDCC)

**Method:**
1. Follow package instructions
2. Usually 1 tablet per liter
3. Wait 30 minutes (or per instructions)
4. May need neutralizing tablet for taste

**Effectiveness:**
- ✅ Kills most bacteria and viruses
- ⚠️ Iodine less effective against Cryptosporidium
- ❌ Does NOT remove chemicals or sediment

**Cost:** $8-15 for 50-100 tablets
**Shelf Life:** 3-5 years sealed
**Weight:** Very light, portable

**Popular Brands:**
- Potable Aqua (iodine)
- Aquatabs (chlorine dioxide)
- Katadyn Micropur

#### Solar Disinfection (SODIS)

**Method:**
1. Use clear plastic bottles (not glass)
2. Fill with water (filter if cloudy)
3. Shake to oxygenate
4. Lay bottles on side in direct sunlight
5. Leave for:
   - 6 hours minimum (bright sun)
   - 2 days (cloudy)

**Effectiveness:**
- ✅ Kills most bacteria
- ✅ Kills most viruses
- ⚠️ Requires clear water and strong UV light
- ❌ Does NOT remove chemicals
- ❌ Does NOT work in freezing temperatures

**Cost:** FREE
**Equipment:** Clear plastic bottles

**WHO Endorsed Method**

### Filtration

#### Personal Water Filters

**LifeStraw/Personal Filters**
- Hollow fiber membrane
- 0.2 micron filter
- Drink directly from source

**Effectiveness:**
- ✅ Removes bacteria (99.9999%)
- ✅ Removes protozoa (99.999%)
- ❌ Does NOT remove viruses (usually)
- ❌ Does NOT remove chemicals

**Cost:** $15-50
**Lifespan:** 1,000-4,000 liters
**Best for:** Personal use, camping, grab-and-go kits

**Popular Brands:**
- LifeStraw
- Sawyer Mini
- Katadyn BeFree

#### Gravity Filters

**Bag/reservoir with filter**
- Fill bag with water
- Hang from tree/hook
- Gravity pulls water through filter
- Collect in clean container

**Effectiveness:** (Depends on filter type)
- ✅ Removes bacteria (99.9999%)
- ✅ Removes protozoa (99.99%)
- ⚠️ Some remove viruses
- ⚠️ Some remove chemicals (activated carbon)

**Cost:** $100-150
**Lifespan:** 10,000-100,000 gallons
**Capacity:** 2-10 liters per fill
**Best for:** Family/group camping, base camp, 2-week to 1-month supplies

**Popular Brands:**
- Sawyer Products
- LifeStraw Mission
- Platypus GravityWorks

#### Pump Filters

**Manual pumping through filter**
- Intake hose in water source
- Pump handle
- Filtered water to container

**Effectiveness:** (Depends on filter)
- ✅ Removes bacteria
- ✅ Removes protozoa
- ⚠️ Some remove viruses
- ⚠️ Carbon filters remove some chemicals

**Cost:** $80-300
**Lifespan:** 1,000-10,000 liters
**Best for:** Faster than gravity, active pumping

**Popular Brands:**
- Katadyn Hiker
- MSR MiniWorks EX
- Survivor Filter

#### Ceramic Filters

**Porous ceramic filter element**
- Can be gravity-fed or pressurized
- Often used in countertop systems
- Can be cleaned and reused

**Effectiveness:**
- ✅ Removes bacteria
- ✅ Removes protozoa
- ✅ Removes sediment
- ⚠️ Some models remove viruses
- ⚠️ Activated carbon version removes some chemicals

**Cost:** $50-300
**Lifespan:** 1,000-5,000 gallons (cleanable/rechargeable)
**Best for:** Home use, long-term preparedness

**Popular Brands:**
- Berkey (very popular in preparedness community)
- Doulton
- ProPur

#### Reverse Osmosis (RO)

**Pressurized membrane filtration**
- Removes nearly everything
- Requires water pressure or pump
- Wastes water (3-5 gallons per 1 gallon produced)

**Effectiveness:**
- ✅ Removes bacteria (99.99%+)
- ✅ Removes viruses (99.99%+)
- ✅ Removes protozoa (99.99%+)
- ✅ Removes chemicals
- ✅ Removes heavy metals
- ✅ Removes salts (can purify seawater!)

**Cost:** $200-500 for home system
**Maintenance:** Replace membranes annually
**Best for:** Long-term home preparedness, contaminated water

**Cons:** Removes beneficial minerals, wastes water

### Multi-Barrier Approach (Best Practice)

**Layer your purification for maximum safety:**

1. **Pre-filter:** Remove sediment (coffee filter, cloth)
2. **Primary treatment:** Boil, chemical, or filter
3. **Secondary treatment:** Different method (redundancy)
4. **Storage:** Clean, covered container

**Example:**
- Cloudy river water
- Pre-filter through cloth
- Filter through ceramic filter
- Add bleach or boil
- Store in clean container

## Finding Water Sources

### Natural Sources

**Safest (Still Purify!)**
- Springs
- Rainfall
- Dew collection

**Use with Caution (Purify!)**
- Streams and rivers (flowing water)
- Lakes and ponds (still water)
- Snowmelt

**Avoid (Last Resort Only)**
- Stagnant water
- Water with odor/color/foam
- Water downstream of industry/farms
- Floodwater

### Urban/Suburban Sources

**In Your Home:**
- Water heater tank (30-50 gallons)
- Toilet tank (NOT bowl) - top tank only if clean
- Pipes (drain from lowest point)
- Ice cubes in freezer
- Canned vegetables (liquid)

**How to Drain Water Heater:**
1. Turn off power/gas to heater
2. Turn off cold water supply valve
3. Attach hose to drain valve at bottom
4. Open hot water tap in house (allows air in)
5. Open drain valve, collect water

**Yield:** 30-50 gallons (must purify)

**Swimming Pools/Hot Tubs:**
- Can use for washing/sanitation
- Do NOT drink (chemicals!)
- Can purify through extensive filtration

### Rainwater Collection

**Simple Method:**
- Clean tarp or plastic sheeting
- Tie corners higher than center (creates basin)
- Collect in clean container
- Purify before drinking

**Permanent System:**
- Gutters on roof
- Downspout to collection tank
- First-flush diverter (diverts initial dirty water)
- Storage tank with overflow
- Filtration system

**Legality:** Check local laws
- Illegal or restricted in some states
- Some areas require permits

### Dew Collection

**Survival Technique (Low Yield):**
1. Tie cloth around ankles
2. Walk through dewy grass early morning
3. Wring out cloth into container
4. Repeat

**Yield:** Small amounts, labor-intensive
**Use:** Survival situation only

## Long-Term Water Solutions

### Wells

**Dug Wells (Shallow)**
- 10-30 feet deep
- Can be dug manually
- More susceptible to contamination
- Lower yield

**Drilled Wells (Deep)**
- 50-400+ feet deep
- Requires professional drilling
- More protected from surface contamination
- Higher yield

**Cost:** $3,000-15,000+ depending on depth
**Considerations:**
- Requires electrical pump (or hand pump backup)
- Regular testing needed
- Maintenance required
- May require permits

**Hand Pump Backup:**
- Allows well use without electricity
- $300-1,500 installed
- Critical for preparedness

### Springs

**Natural groundwater source**
- Must test for safety
- Can be developed for consistent flow
- Often requires land ownership or permission
- Free flowing water

**Spring Development:**
1. Identify spring source
2. Test water quality
3. Create collection box
4. Pipe to storage or use point

**Cost:** $500-5,000 for development
**Benefit:** Gravity-fed, no power needed

### Rainwater Harvesting (Detailed)

**System Components:**

1. **Catchment Surface (Roof)**
   - Metal or tile preferred
   - Avoid asphalt shingles (chemicals)
   - Clean regularly

2. **Gutters and Downspouts**
   - Keep clean (leaf guards)
   - Proper pitch for flow
   - Screen to prevent debris

3. **First-Flush Diverter**
   - Diverts first rain (washes roof)
   - Usually first 10-20 gallons
   - Critical for water quality

4. **Storage Tank**
   - Food-grade material
   - Covered (prevent algae, mosquitoes)
   - Overflow valve
   - Drain valve at bottom

5. **Filtration/Purification**
   - Sediment filter
   - Carbon filter
   - UV light or other purification
   - Multi-stage is best

**Collection Potential:**
Formula: `Roof Area (sq ft) × Rainfall (inches) × 0.623 = Gallons`

Example: 1,000 sq ft roof × 1 inch rain × 0.623 = 623 gallons

**Annual Example (40 inches rain/year):**
1,000 sq ft roof = 24,920 gallons/year

### Surface Water Filtration Systems

**For properties with pond/stream:**

**Intake System:**
- Screened intake away from shore
- Multiple depths (seasonal variation)
- Pre-filtration

**Treatment:**
1. Sediment filter (removes particles)
2. Activated carbon (removes chemicals, improves taste)
3. UV sterilization (kills biological)
4. Storage tank

**Cost:** $1,000-5,000 for complete system
**Maintenance:** Regular filter replacement, UV bulb annually

## Water Conservation

**Make Your Water Last Longer:**

### Drinking Water
- Use minimum needed
- Don't waste when purifying
- Share drinking vessels (family only)
- Drink enough to stay hydrated (don't ration to dangerous levels)

### Washing/Hygiene
- Use wet wipes instead of washing (short-term)
- Two-bucket method (soapy/rinse)
- Reuse water when possible
- Wash only when necessary

### Cooking
- Use minimal water for cooking
- Save cooking water for washing
- Choose foods requiring less water
- One-pot meals

### Toilet
- "If it's yellow, let it mellow"
- Use greywater for flushing
- Consider composting toilet for long-term

### Greywater Reuse
**Greywater:** Water from sinks, showers, washing (NOT toilet)

**Safe Uses:**
- Flushing toilets
- Watering plants (non-edible)
- Washing floors

**Unsafe Uses:**
- Drinking
- Cooking
- Watering vegetables

## Water Quality Testing

### When to Test

- Before drinking from new source
- Regularly from wells/springs (annually minimum)
- After flooding or contamination event
- If water appearance/taste/odor changes
- If anyone becomes ill

### What to Test For

**Basic Tests:**
- Bacteria (coliform)
- pH
- Hardness
- Nitrates

**Comprehensive Tests:**
- Heavy metals (lead, arsenic, mercury)
- Pesticides
- Industrial contaminants
- Volatile organic compounds (VOCs)

### How to Test

**Home Test Kits:**
- Basic bacteria tests
- pH strips
- Available at hardware stores
- $10-50

**Mail-In Laboratory Testing:**
- Most comprehensive
- Various test packages
- $50-300 depending on what's tested

**Professional Testing:**
- County health department (often free or low-cost)
- Certified laboratories
- Most accurate

## Troubleshooting Common Issues

### Stored Water Smells Bad
**Cause:** Algae growth, bacterial contamination
**Solution:**
- Discard water
- Clean container with bleach solution
- Ensure containers are opaque or in dark location
- Add water preserver when refilling

### Water Tastes Like Plastic
**Cause:** New containers, non-food-grade plastic
**Solution:**
- Ensure food-grade containers only
- Rinse containers multiple times
- Add activated carbon filter
- Taste should dissipate over time

### Water Turns Cloudy
**Cause:** Mineral precipitation, contamination
**Solution:**
- If minerals, safe to drink but filter
- If contamination suspected, purify before use
- Check storage conditions

### Filter Clogs Quickly
**Cause:** High sediment in water
**Solution:**
- Pre-filter through cloth/coffee filter
- Let sediment settle before filtering
- Clean/backflush filter regularly

### Can't Get Water Out of Storage Barrel
**Cause:** No siphon or pump
**Solution:**
- Install spigot at bottom of barrel (best)
- Use siphon pump
- Tip carefully (very heavy!)
