## Life-Threatening Emergencies (ABCs)

### The ABCs - Check in Order

**A = Airway**
- Is airway open and clear?
- Look for: Choking, tongue blocking airway, foreign objects

**B = Breathing**
- Is person breathing?
- Look, listen, feel for breath (5-10 seconds)

**C = Circulation**
- Is heart beating?
- Check pulse (carotid artery in neck)
- Look for severe bleeding

**If any ABC fails → Immediate action required**

---

### CPR (Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation)

**When to Use:**
- Person not breathing AND no pulse
- Drowning victim
- Cardiac arrest
- Electrocution

**Adult CPR (Age 8+):**

1. **Check responsiveness:** Tap shoulders, shout "Are you okay?"
2. **Call 911** (or send someone)
3. **Position:** Flat on back on firm surface
4. **Hand position:** Center of chest, between nipples
5. **Compressions:**
   - Place heel of one hand on chest
   - Other hand on top, fingers interlaced
   - Arms straight, shoulders over hands
   - Push hard and fast: 2 inches deep
   - Rate: 100-120 per minute (beat of "Stayin' Alive")
   - Count: 30 compressions
6. **Rescue breaths (if trained):**
   - Tilt head back, lift chin
   - Pinch nose shut
   - Full seal over mouth
   - 2 breaths (1 second each, chest should rise)
7. **Continue:** 30 compressions, 2 breaths
8. **Don't stop** until: Person breathes, help arrives, or you're too exhausted

**Child CPR (1-8 years):**
- Same as adult but:
- One or two hands for compressions
- Compress 2 inches deep
- Gentler breaths (until chest rises)

**Infant CPR (under 1 year):**
- Two fingers for compressions (center of chest, just below nipple line)
- Compress 1.5 inches deep
- Gentle puffs of air (cheek full of air)
- Cover infant's mouth AND nose with your mouth

**Compression-Only CPR:**
- If untrained or uncomfortable with breaths
- Continuous chest compressions at 100-120/min
- Better than doing nothing
- Effective for first several minutes

**Important:**
- Don't stop to check pulse during CPR
- Switch rescuers every 2 minutes if possible (exhaustion)
- AED (automated external defibrillator) if available - follow voice prompts

---

### Choking (Heimlich Maneuver)

**Signs of Choking:**
- Can't speak or cough
- Hands clutching throat (universal sign)
- Silent or high-pitched wheezing
- Face turning blue

**Conscious Adult/Child:**

1. **Ask:** "Are you choking?" (if can't speak → choking)
2. **Stand behind person**
3. **Make a fist** with one hand
4. **Position fist:** Thumb side against abdomen, just above navel
5. **Grab fist** with other hand
6. **Thrust inward and upward** (J-shaped motion)
7. **Repeat** until object expelled or person becomes unconscious
8. **Each thrust** should be distinct and forceful

**Unconscious Person:**
- Lower to ground
- Begin CPR (compressions may dislodge object)
- Before giving breaths, look in mouth for object
- If visible and easy to remove → sweep out with finger
- Continue CPR

**Self-Heimlich:**
- Make fist above navel
- Grab with other hand
- Thrust inward/upward repeatedly
- OR lean over chair/counter edge, press abdomen forcefully

**Infant Choking (under 1 year):**

1. **Sit down, hold infant face-down on forearm**
2. **Support head/neck** with hand
3. **5 back blows** between shoulder blades (heel of hand)
4. **Flip infant face-up** on other forearm
5. **5 chest thrusts** (two fingers, center of chest, like infant CPR)
6. **Repeat** 5 back blows, 5 chest thrusts until object expelled
7. **Never** blind finger sweep in infant mouth

---

### Severe Allergic Reaction (Anaphylaxis)

**Signs:**
- Difficulty breathing / wheezing
- Swelling of face, lips, tongue, throat
- Hives / widespread rash
- Rapid pulse
- Dizziness / loss of consciousness
- Nausea / vomiting
- Sense of impending doom

**Action:**
1. **Call 911 immediately**
2. **Use EpiPen (epinephrine auto-injector) if available:**
   - Remove safety cap
   - Jab firmly into outer thigh (through clothing OK)
   - Hold 3-10 seconds (depends on device)
   - Massage area 10 seconds
3. **Lay person down** (unless vomiting/breathing difficulty → sit up)
4. **Second dose** may be needed after 5-15 minutes if no improvement
5. **Even if improved**, must go to hospital (reaction can return)

**Stockpile Considerations:**
- EpiPen prescription: 2-pack (~$600, or generic ~$100-300)
- Shelf life: 12-18 months
- Store at room temperature (not hot car/freezer)
- Train all family members on use

## Bleeding Control

### Minor Bleeding

**Action:**
1. **Wash hands** (if possible)
2. **Rinse wound** with clean water
3. **Apply direct pressure** with clean cloth (5-10 minutes)
4. **Clean wound** thoroughly (soap and water)
5. **Apply antibiotic ointment** (Neosporin, Bacitracin)
6. **Cover with bandage**
7. **Change daily** and check for infection

---

### Severe Bleeding (Life-Threatening)

**Recognize:**
- Blood spurting from wound
- Blood soaking through bandages
- Loss of consciousness possible

**Action - Direct Pressure:**
1. **Expose wound** (cut away clothing if needed)
2. **Apply direct pressure** with cloth/gauze
3. **Press hard** - don't worry about hurting them
4. **Hold pressure 10+ minutes** without peeking
5. **If blood soaks through** → add more cloth on top (don't remove first layer)
6. **Keep pressure** until bleeding stops or help arrives

**If Direct Pressure Fails:**

**Pressure Points:**
- **Arm bleeding:** Press brachial artery (inside of upper arm, between bicep and bone)
- **Leg bleeding:** Press femoral artery (groin crease, where thigh meets abdomen)

**Tourniquet (Last Resort):**
- Use ONLY if: Severe limb bleeding, direct pressure fails, life-threatening
- Commercial tourniquet (CAT, SOFTT) best
- Improvised: Belt, torn clothing (at least 1.5" wide)

**How to Apply:**
1. **Position:** 2-3 inches above wound (between wound and heart)
2. **Wrap** around limb twice
3. **Tie tight** overhand knot
4. **Insert stick/rod** through knot
5. **Twist** until bleeding stops (will be very painful)
6. **Secure** stick so it can't unwind
7. **Mark time** on person's forehead (permanent marker)
8. **Do NOT remove** - only hospital should remove
9. **Do NOT loosen** or cover with blanket (need to monitor)

**Tourniquet Notes:**
- May result in loss of limb (better than loss of life)
- Limb can survive 2 hours with tourniquet
- Bleeding control > limb preservation in life-threatening situation

---

### Internal Bleeding (Suspect After Trauma)

**Signs:**
- Bruising / discoloration spreading
- Swollen, firm abdomen
- Vomiting or coughing up blood
- Blood in urine or stool (black, tarry)
- Weak, rapid pulse
- Cold, clammy skin
- Confusion, faintness

**Action:**
1. **Call 911 immediately**
2. **Treat for shock** (see below)
3. **Keep person still** (don't move if spine injury possible)
4. **Nothing by mouth** (no food or water)
5. **Monitor breathing and pulse**
6. **Evacuate to hospital ASAP**

## Shock

**What is Shock:**
- Life-threatening condition
- Body not getting enough blood/oxygen
- Can result from: Severe bleeding, heart attack, allergic reaction, infection, trauma

**Signs:**
- Pale, cold, clammy skin
- Rapid, weak pulse
- Rapid, shallow breathing
- Weakness, dizziness
- Confusion, anxiety
- Nausea / vomiting
- Thirst
- Loss of consciousness

**Treatment:**
1. **Call 911 / evacuate to hospital**
2. **Lay person down** on back
3. **Elevate legs 12 inches** (unless: head/neck/spine/leg injury, breathing difficulty)
4. **Maintain body temperature** (blanket under and over, but don't overheat)
5. **Nothing by mouth** (no food, water, smoking)
6. **Monitor ABCs** continuously
7. **Reassure** (calm, comfort)
8. **Treat cause** (stop bleeding, etc.)

## Burns

### Burn Degrees

**First-Degree (Superficial):**
- Red, painful
- No blisters
- Example: Mild sunburn
- Heals: 3-6 days

**Second-Degree (Partial Thickness):**
- Red, very painful
- Blisters
- Swelling
- Example: Severe sunburn, scalding
- Heals: 2-3 weeks

**Third-Degree (Full Thickness):**
- White, brown, or charred
- Painless (nerves destroyed)
- Leathery appearance
- Requires medical care
- Heals: Months, often requires grafts

---

### Burn Treatment

**Immediate Actions (All Burns):**
1. **Stop the burning:**
   - Heat: Remove from source
   - Chemical: Brush off powder, flush 20 minutes
   - Electrical: Ensure power off before touching
2. **Remove jewelry/tight clothing** before swelling (don't remove stuck clothing)
3. **Cool the burn:**
   - Cool (not ice cold) running water 10-20 minutes
   - OR cool compress
   - Don't use ice (causes more damage)

**First-Degree Burns:**
1. Cool with water
2. Aloe vera gel or moisturizing lotion
3. Cover loosely if needed
4. Pain relief: Ibuprofen or acetaminophen
5. Keep out of sun

**Second-Degree Burns (Small, <3 inches):**
1. Cool with water 10-20 minutes
2. Gently clean with mild soap and water
3. **Don't break blisters** (infection risk)
4. If blister breaks: Clean, antibiotic ointment, cover
5. Apply antibiotic ointment (after cooled)
6. Cover with non-stick gauze and bandage
7. Change dressing daily
8. Pain relief: Ibuprofen or acetaminophen
9. Watch for infection signs

**Seek Medical Care If:**
- Third-degree burn (any size)
- Second-degree burn larger than 3 inches
- Burns on face, hands, feet, genitals, major joints
- Chemical or electrical burn
- Breathing difficulty (smoke inhalation)
- Signs of infection (increasing pain, pus, fever, red streaks)

**DO NOT:**
- Apply ice (damages tissue)
- Apply butter, oils, ointments immediately (traps heat)
- Break blisters intentionally
- Use fluffy cotton (fibers stick)

---

### Infection Signs (All Wounds)

**Watch for:**
- Increasing pain (after first 2 days)
- Redness spreading outward
- Red streaks from wound toward heart
- Increased swelling
- Pus (yellow/green discharge)
- Warmth around wound
- Fever over 100.4°F
- Swollen lymph nodes

**Action:**
- Clean wound thoroughly
- Apply antibiotic ointment
- Change dressing twice daily
- If worsening or fever → seek medical care (antibiotics may be needed)

## Fractures & Sprains

### Fractures (Broken Bones)

**Signs:**
- Heard or felt a snap
- Severe pain
- Swelling, bruising
- Deformity (bent at odd angle)
- Can't move or bear weight
- Bone visible (open fracture)

**Treatment:**

**DO NOT move person if:**
- Suspect spine/neck injury
- Unconscious
- Multiple injuries

**General Fracture Care:**
1. **Stabilize** - Don't try to straighten
2. **Immobilize** above and below fracture
3. **Splint** (see below)
4. **Ice** wrapped in cloth (20 min on, 20 min off)
5. **Elevate** if possible
6. **Pain relief** - Ibuprofen or acetaminophen
7. **Monitor circulation** - Check fingers/toes every 30 min (warm, pink, can feel?)
8. **Seek medical care**

**Open Fracture (Bone Through Skin):**
- Life-threatening - infection risk
- **Don't push bone back in**
- Cover wound with sterile dressing
- Immobilize
- Treat for shock
- Evacuate immediately

---

### Splinting

**Purpose:** Immobilize injury to prevent further damage

**Principles:**
- Immobilize joint above AND below injury
- Splint in position found (don't straighten unless compromised circulation)
- Pad splint (prevent pressure sores)
- Check circulation after splinting

**Improvised Splints:**
- Sticks/branches
- Rolled newspapers/magazines
- Cardboard
- Foam pad
- Pillow (ankle)
- Opposite leg (tie injured leg to good leg)

**How to Splint:**
1. **Pad** splint material
2. **Position** splint to immobilize joints above and below
3. **Secure** with bandages, strips of cloth, belts (not too tight)
4. **Check circulation** (pink, warm, can feel?)
5. **Recheck** every 30 minutes

**Arm/Wrist:**
- Splint from elbow to past hand
- Sling to support (tie around neck)

**Leg/Ankle:**
- Splint from above knee to past foot
- OR pillow around ankle, tape secure

---

### Sprains & Strains

**Sprain:** Stretched/torn ligament (connects bones)
**Strain:** Stretched/torn muscle or tendon (connects muscle to bone)

**Signs:**
- Pain, swelling
- Bruising
- Limited movement
- Popping sensation (sprains)

**Treatment - RICE:**

**R = Rest**
- Don't use injured area
- Crutches for ankle/knee sprains

**I = Ice**
- 20 minutes at a time
- 20 minutes off between applications
- First 48-72 hours
- Use barrier (cloth) between ice and skin

**C = Compression**
- Wrap with elastic bandage
- Firm but not tight (shouldn't cut off circulation)
- Loosen if: Numbness, tingling, increased pain, swelling below wrap

**E = Elevation**
- Raise above heart level
- Reduces swelling
- As much as possible for first 48 hours

**Pain Relief:**
- Ibuprofen (reduces inflammation)
- Acetaminophen (if can't take ibuprofen)

**Recovery:**
- Mild sprain: 1-2 weeks
- Moderate: 3-6 weeks
- Severe: Months

**Seek Medical Care If:**
- Can't bear weight
- Joint feels unstable
- Numbness
- Severe pain or swelling
- No improvement after 3 days

## Wounds & Infections

### Wound Cleaning (Critical Skill)

**Proper cleaning prevents 90% of infections**

**Supplies:**
- Clean water (boiled and cooled, or bottled)
- Soap (mild, unscented)
- Clean cloth or gauze
- Tweezers (sterilized)
- Antibiotic ointment
- Bandages

**Steps:**
1. **Wash YOUR hands** thoroughly (soap, 20 seconds)
2. **Stop bleeding** (direct pressure 5-10 minutes)
3. **Rinse wound** under running water 5 minutes (removes debris)
4. **Clean around wound** with soap and water (not in wound)
5. **Remove debris** with sterilized tweezers if needed
6. **Irrigate wound** with clean water (syringe if available)
7. **Pat dry** with clean cloth
8. **Apply antibiotic ointment** thin layer
9. **Cover with bandage**

**Deep or Large Wounds:**
- May need stitches if:
  - Edges don't come together
  - Deeper than 1/4 inch
  - On face, joints, or over bone
  - Caused by animal/human bite
- Seek medical care within 6-8 hours (best results)

---

### Wound Closure (If Medical Care Unavailable)

**Butterfly Bandages / Steri-Strips:**
1. Clean wound thoroughly
2. Stop bleeding completely
3. Bring edges together gently
4. Apply strips perpendicular to wound
5. Start at middle, work outward
6. Leave small gaps between strips (drainage)
7. Don't remove for 7-10 days

**Superglue (Medical-Grade Dermabond or Regular):**
- For small, clean cuts only
- Apply to DRY skin
- Bring edges together
- Apply thin line along (not in) wound
- Hold edges 60 seconds
- Forms waterproof seal
- Peels off naturally in 5-10 days

**DO NOT use superglue if:**
- Puncture wound
- Animal/human bite
- Dirty wound
- Signs of infection
- Deep wound

---

### Puncture Wounds

**Danger:** Deep, high infection risk (tetanus)

**Treatment:**
1. **Don't remove object if large** (knife, stake) - stabilize and seek help
2. **Small punctures:** Let bleed briefly (cleans wound)
3. **Clean thoroughly** - irrigate with water
4. **Soak in warm water** 15 minutes, 3-4 times daily
5. **Watch closely for infection**
6. **Tetanus shot** if not current (within 10 years, or 5 years for dirty wound)

**High Risk Punctures:**
- Stepping on nail
- Animal bites
- Stab wounds
- Deep wood splinters

---

### Animal & Human Bites

**High infection risk** - bacteria from mouth

**Treatment:**
1. **Wash immediately** under running water 10+ minutes
2. **Clean with soap** thoroughly
3. **Apply antibiotic ointment**
4. **Don't close wound** (needs to drain)
5. **Seek medical care** (may need antibiotics, tetanus, rabies evaluation)

**Rabies Risk:**
- Wild animals: Bats, raccoons, skunks, foxes (HIGH RISK)
- Domestic dogs/cats: Lower risk if vaccinated
- If bitten by wild animal → rabies post-exposure shots immediately (fatal if untreated)

## Environmental Emergencies

### Heat Exhaustion

**Causes:** Exercise/work in hot environment, dehydration

**Signs:**
- Heavy sweating
- Weakness, dizziness
- Headache
- Nausea
- Pale, cool, clammy skin
- Normal temperature or slightly elevated

**Treatment:**
1. **Move to cool area** (shade, AC)
2. **Lay down**
3. **Loosen clothing**
4. **Sip water** slowly (16-32 oz over 1 hour)
5. **Cool with wet cloths** or spray bottle
6. **Rest**

**Usually improves within 30 minutes**

---

### Heat Stroke (LIFE-THREATENING)

**Causes:** Body can't cool itself, temperature 104°F+

**Signs:**
- HIGH body temperature (104°F+)
- Hot, DRY skin (or still sweating)
- Rapid pulse
- Confusion, disorientation
- Loss of consciousness
- Seizures possible

**Treatment:**
1. **Call 911 immediately**
2. **Move to cool area**
3. **Cool RAPIDLY:**
   - Remove excess clothing
   - Spray with water, fan aggressively
   - Ice packs on neck, armpits, groin
   - Wet sheets
4. **Monitor ABCs**
5. **Don't give fluids if unconscious**

**Every minute counts - permanent damage/death possible**

---

### Hypothermia

**Causes:** Prolonged cold exposure (doesn't have to be freezing)

**Mild Hypothermia (95-90°F):**
- Shivering (intense)
- Confusion, slurred speech
- Clumsy, stumbling
- Apathy

**Severe Hypothermia (<90°F):**
- Shivering stops
- Rigid muscles
- Slow pulse and breathing
- Loss of consciousness
- Appears dead

**Treatment:**

**Mild:**
1. **Move to shelter**
2. **Remove wet clothing**
3. **Warm gradually:**
   - Blankets, sleeping bag
   - Warm (not hot) drinks if conscious
   - Body heat (skin-to-skin in sleeping bag)
4. **Dry clothing**
5. **Hat** (major heat loss from head)

**Severe:**
1. **Call 911 / evacuate**
2. **Handle GENTLY** (rough movement can trigger cardiac arrest)
3. **Move to shelter carefully**
4. **Remove wet clothing** (cut off if needed)
5. **Insulate from ground**
6. **Wrap in blankets**
7. **Apply heat packs** to core (chest, neck, armpits, groin) NOT limbs
8. **Monitor breathing** - may need CPR
9. **Don't give fluids if unconscious**

**DO NOT:**
- Rub skin (damages tissue)
- Apply direct heat (heating pad, fire) to limbs
- Give alcohol
- Assume person is dead (can appear dead but survive)

---

### Frostbite

**Causes:** Freezing of skin and tissue (fingers, toes, nose, ears)

**Signs:**
- Numbness
- White/grayish-yellow skin
- Hard, waxy feeling
- Blisters (severe cases)

**Treatment:**
1. **Get to warm location**
2. **Don't walk on frostbitten feet** (causes more damage)
3. **Remove wet clothing/jewelry**
4. **Warm gradually** in warm (NOT hot) water (98-105°F) for 15-30 minutes
5. **Extremely painful** as warming (give pain meds)
6. **Don't rub** (damages tissue)
7. **Protect blisters** - don't pop
8. **Wrap in sterile dressing** (separate fingers/toes)
9. **Seek medical care**

**DO NOT rewarm if risk of refreezing** (worse damage)

## 72-Hour First Aid Kit

**Container:** Waterproof bag or plastic tote

**Bandages & Wound Care:**
- [ ] Adhesive bandages (Band-Aids): 50 assorted sizes
- [ ] Gauze pads (4x4"): 25 count
- [ ] Rolled gauze: 6 rolls
- [ ] Medical tape: 2 rolls
- [ ] Elastic bandage (ACE wrap): 2
- [ ] Triangular bandage: 2 (sling, large wound)
- [ ] Butterfly bandages/Steri-Strips: 20 count

**Instruments:**
- [ ] Scissors (trauma shears): 1
- [ ] Tweezers: 1
- [ ] Safety pins: 12
- [ ] Thermometer (digital): 1

**Medications:**
- [ ] Pain relief (ibuprofen 200mg): 100 tablets
- [ ] Pain relief (acetaminophen 500mg): 100 tablets
- [ ] Antihistamine (Benadryl 25mg): 50 tablets
- [ ] Anti-diarrhea (Imodium): 24 tablets
- [ ] Antacid (Tums): 1 bottle
- [ ] Aspirin 325mg: 50 tablets (heart attack)

**Topical:**
- [ ] Antibiotic ointment: 3 tubes
- [ ] Hydrocortisone cream 1%: 1 tube (rash, itch)
- [ ] Burn gel/aloe vera: 1 tube

**Protection:**
- [ ] Disposable gloves (nitrile): 10 pairs
- [ ] CPR face shield: 2
- [ ] Hand sanitizer: 1 bottle (8 oz)
- [ ] Antiseptic wipes: 50 count

**Other:**
- [ ] Emergency blanket (Mylar): 2
- [ ] Cold packs (instant): 4
- [ ] First aid manual (printed): 1
- [ ] Emergency contact card: 1
- [ ] Pencil and notepad

**Cost:** $75-125

## Long-Term Medical Supplies

### 3-6 Month Considerations

**Medications:**
- Work with doctor to stockpile prescription meds
- Rotate as use (FIFO)
- Store properly (cool, dark, dry)

**Advanced Supplies:**
- Suture kit (if trained): $30-60
- Blood pressure cuff: $25-50
- Stethoscope: $20-40
- Tourniquet (CAT or SOFTT): $30 each (2-3 recommended)

- Israeli bandage (pressure bandage): $10 each (3-5 recommended)

**Reference Materials:**
- Medical handbook (Where There Is No Doctor - free PDF)
- First aid manual (comprehensive)
- Prescription medication guide
