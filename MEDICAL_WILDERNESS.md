## Delayed Evacuation Scenarios

**When Medical Help is Hours/Days Away:**
- Remote wilderness (backcountry)
- Natural disaster (roads blocked)
- Grid-down scenario
- Rural/mountain isolation (winter)

**Your Goals:**
1. **Stabilize** - Prevent condition from worsening
2. **Monitor** - Watch for complications
3. **Document** - Record vital signs, treatments
4. **Decide** - Evacuate vs. care in place

## Monitoring Vital Signs

**Essential Skill for Extended Care**

### Normal Ranges (Adults)

**Temperature:**
- Normal: 97.0-99.0°F (36.1-37.2°C)
- Low fever: 99.1-100.4°F
- Fever: 100.5-102.9°F
- High fever: 103°F+
- Dangerous: 105°F+ (brain damage risk)

**Pulse (Heart Rate):**
- Resting adult: 60-100 bpm
- Athletes: 40-60 bpm
- Children: 70-120 bpm
- Infants: 100-160 bpm

**Respiratory Rate:**
- Adult: 12-20 breaths/min
- Child: 20-30 breaths/min
- Infant: 30-60 breaths/min

**Blood Pressure:**
- Normal: 120/80 or below
- Elevated: 120-129/<80
- High: 130/80 or higher
- Low: 90/60 or lower (shock concern)

### How to Check

**Pulse:**
1. Two fingers on wrist (thumb side) or neck (carotid)
2. Count beats for 15 seconds
3. Multiply by 4
4. Note: Strong/weak, regular/irregular

**Respiration:**
1. Watch chest rise/fall
2. Count for 30 seconds, multiply by 2
3. Note: Labored, shallow, normal

**Temperature:**
- Digital thermometer (oral, armpit, rectal)
- Oral: Most common (wait 10 min after eating/drinking)
- Rectal: Most accurate (add 1°F to oral equivalent)
- Armpit: Least accurate (subtract 1°F from oral)

**Level of Consciousness:**
- **Alert:** Awake, oriented (person, place, time)
- **Verbal:** Responds to voice
- **Pain:** Only responds to painful stimuli
- **Unresponsive:** No response
- (AVPU scale)

### Documentation

**Record Every 4-8 Hours (or more if critical):**
- Date/time
- Temperature, pulse, respiration, blood pressure
- Level of consciousness
- Pain level (1-10 scale)
- Treatments given
- Food/water intake
- Urine output
- Any changes

**Why:** Tracks improvement/decline, critical for medical handoff

## Advanced Wound Management

### Wound Irrigation (Critical Skill)

**Purpose:** Remove bacteria, debris (prevents infection)

**Supplies:**
- Clean water (boiled and cooled, or sterile saline)
- Irrigation syringe (60ml) or squeeze bottle
- Clean basin
- Gauze

**Technique:**
1. **Wash your hands** thoroughly
2. **Fill syringe** with clean water/saline
3. **Position wound** over basin (catch runoff)
4. **Spray forcefully** into wound (2-3 inches away)
5. **Use entire syringe** (60ml minimum, more for dirty wounds)
6. **Refill and repeat** 3-5 times (300-500ml total)
7. **Pat dry** with sterile gauze
8. **Dress wound**

**High-pressure irrigation removes 10x more bacteria than rinsing**

---

### Wound Debridement (Removing Dead Tissue)

**When Needed:**
- Dead/black tissue visible
- Wound not healing (week 2+)
- Signs of infection despite treatment

**Supplies:**
- Sterile scissors or scalpel
- Tweezers
- Sterile gauze
- Clean water
- Antibiotic ointment

**Technique (Basic):**
1. **Pain management** (ibuprofen before, 30-60 min)
2. **Clean wound** thoroughly (irrigation)
3. **Identify dead tissue** (black, gray, hard, or mushy)
4. **Cut away** only obviously dead tissue (bleeds = alive, don't cut)
5. **Irrigate again** after debridement
6. **Apply antibiotic ointment**
7. **Dress wound**

**Warning:** Advanced skill - practice on raw chicken first if possible

---

### Managing Infected Wounds

**Signs of Infection:**
- Increasing pain after day 2
- Redness spreading (draw outline with marker, track spread)
- Swelling
- Warmth
- Pus (yellow/green/gray discharge)
- Fever
- Red streaks toward heart (lymphangitis - serious)
- Foul smell

**Treatment:**

**1. Aggressive Cleaning:**
- Remove bandage
- Irrigate wound 3-4x daily (300-500ml each time)
- Debride any dead tissue
- Apply antibiotic ointment
- Fresh bandage

**2. Warm Soaks:**
- Warm water (not hot)
- Soak 15-20 minutes, 3-4x daily
- Promotes drainage, increases blood flow

**3. Elevation:**
- Keep injured area elevated above heart
- Reduces swelling

**4. Antibiotics (If Available):**
- Oral antibiotics critical for spreading infection
- See MEDICAL_MEDICATIONS.md for stockpile options
- Common: Amoxicillin, cephalexin, doxycycline

**5. Monitor Closely:**
- Check vital signs every 4-6 hours
- Watch for systemic infection (fever, confusion, rapid pulse)
- Mark red area with marker, check spread every 12 hours

**Red Flags (Evacuate Immediately if Possible):**
- Red streaks spreading rapidly
- Fever over 103°F
- Confusion, disorientation
- Rapid pulse/breathing
- Dark/foul-smelling drainage
- Skin breakdown around wound

### Abscess Drainage

**Abscess:** Pocket of pus under skin (boil)

**Signs:**
- Red, swollen, warm lump
- Very painful
- May see white/yellow center
- Feels "squishy" or fluctuant

**Treatment:**

**Option 1: Encourage Natural Drainage**
- Warm compresses 20 min, 4x daily
- May open on its own in 3-7 days
- If opens: Clean, apply antibiotic ointment, cover

**Option 2: Incision & Drainage (I&D)**
- Only if: Large, very painful, not draining naturally
- Last resort (infection risk)

**I&D Procedure:**
1. **Sterilize blade** (lighter flame, let cool, or alcohol)
2. **Clean skin** around abscess thoroughly
3. **Make small incision** at "ripest" point (usually center)
4. **Gentle pressure** around abscess (squeeze pus out)
5. **Irrigate cavity** with clean water/saline
6. **Pack lightly** with gauze wick (allows continued drainage)
7. **Cover** with bandage
8. **Remove packing** after 24 hours
9. **Continue warm soaks** and cleaning until healed

**After I&D:**
- Watch for signs of worsening infection
- Oral antibiotics if available
- Healing time: 1-2 weeks

## Common Illnesses & Treatments

### Diarrhea & Vomiting

**Dangers:**
- Dehydration (life-threatening if severe)
- Electrolyte imbalance

**Treatment:**

**1. Prevent Dehydration:**
- **Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS):**
  - Best: WHO formula (see recipe below)
  - Good: Pedialyte, Gatorade diluted 50/50 with water
  - OK: Coconut water
- **Sip frequently:** Small amounts every 15 min (4-8 oz)
- **Goal:** Replace losses (input = output)

**DIY Oral Rehydration Solution:**
- 6 teaspoons sugar
- 1/2 teaspoon salt
- 1 liter (4 cups) clean water
- Optional: 1/2 teaspoon salt substitute (potassium)
- Mix thoroughly, sip throughout day

**2. Medications:**
- **Anti-diarrhea (Imodium):** Slows diarrhea (don't use if bloody diarrhea or fever)
- **Anti-nausea (Dramamine, Zofran):** Stops vomiting
- **Pepto-Bismol:** Both diarrhea and nausea

**3. Diet:**
- **During illness:** Clear liquids only
- **As improving:** BRAT diet (Bananas, Rice, Applesauce, Toast)
- **Avoid:** Dairy, fatty foods, caffeine, alcohol

**4. When to Worry:**
- Blood in stool
- High fever (>102°F)
- Severe abdominal pain
- Signs of dehydration (see below)
- Lasting >3 days

**Dehydration Signs:**
- Decreased urination (dark yellow)
- Dry mouth, cracked lips
- Sunken eyes
- Dizziness when standing
- Confusion
- Skin "tenting" (pinch skin, stays pinched)

---

### Respiratory Infections (Cold, Flu, Bronchitis)

**Symptoms:**
- Cough, congestion
- Sore throat
- Fever
- Fatigue
- Body aches

**Treatment (Supportive - No Cure):**

**1. Rest:** Body needs energy to fight infection

**2. Hydration:** 8-12 cups fluid daily (water, tea, broth)

**3. Fever Management:**
- Ibuprofen or acetaminophen (dosing per package)
- Cool cloths if uncomfortable
- Don't treat low fever aggressively (fever helps fight infection)

**4. Cough:**
- Honey (1 tablespoon as needed, not for infants <1 year)
- Dextromethorphan (Robitussin DM)
- Steam inhalation (towel over head, bowl of hot water)

**5. Congestion:**
- Saline nasal rinse (neti pot)
- Pseudoephedrine (Sudafed) - decongestant
- Humidifier (or steam from shower)

**6. Sore Throat:**
- Salt water gargle (1/2 tsp salt in 8 oz warm water)
- Honey
- Throat lozenges

**Antibiotics Don't Help Viral Infections (most colds/flu)**

**When Antibiotics ARE Needed (Bacterial Infection):**
- Strep throat (white patches, severe pain, fever)
- Sinus infection lasting >10 days
- Ear infection (severe pain, fever)
- Pneumonia (see below)

---

### Pneumonia (Serious)

**Signs:**
- Productive cough (green/yellow/bloody mucus)
- High fever (>102°F)
- Chest pain when breathing/coughing
- Shortness of breath
- Rapid breathing
- Confusion (elderly)

**Treatment:**
1. **Antibiotics** (critical - azithromycin, amoxicillin, doxycycline)
2. **Fluids** (dehydration common)
3. **Fever control**
4. **Rest** (semi-upright position easier to breathe)
5. **Monitor breathing** (respiratory rate, oxygen levels if equipment available)

**Danger Signs:**
- Breathing rate >30/min
- Confusion
- Bluish lips/nails
- Can't complete sentence without gasping
- → **Evacuate if possible**

---

### Urinary Tract Infection (UTI)

**Signs:**
- Burning with urination
- Frequent urge to urinate
- Lower abdominal pain
- Cloudy or bloody urine
- Strong-smelling urine

**Treatment:**

**1. Antibiotics:** Required (won't clear without)
- Nitrofurantoin (Macrobid) - first choice
- Sulfamethoxazole/trimethoprim (Bactrim)
- Ciprofloxacin (Cipro)
- Course: 3-7 days

**2. Hydration:** Drink 8-12 cups water daily (flushes bacteria)

**3. Cranberry:** Unsweetened cranberry juice (may help, not cure)

**4. Pain Relief:**
- Phenazopyridine (AZO, Pyridium) - urinary pain reliever
- Ibuprofen

**Untreated UTI Can Progress to Kidney Infection (Serious):**
- Fever, chills
- Back/side pain
- Nausea, vomiting
- Needs stronger antibiotics

---

### Skin Infections (Cellulitis, Impetigo)

**Cellulitis:** Bacterial skin infection (deeper layers)

**Signs:**
- Red, swollen, warm area
- Spreading rapidly
- Pain/tenderness
- Fever

**Treatment:**
- Oral antibiotics (cephalexin, amoxicillin/clavulanate)
- Elevation
- Mark borders with marker (track spread)
- Warm compresses
- Course: 7-10 days

**Impetigo:** Surface skin infection (common in children)

**Signs:**
- Crusty, honey-colored sores
- Usually around mouth/nose
- Contagious

**Treatment:**
- Topical antibiotic ointment (Mupirocin - prescription)
- OR oral antibiotics if widespread
- Keep clean, wash hands frequently

## Dental Emergencies

### Severe Toothache

**Causes:** Cavity, abscess, cracked tooth

**Treatment:**

**1. Pain Control:**
- Ibuprofen 400-600mg every 6 hours (best for dental pain)
- Acetaminophen 500-1000mg every 6 hours
- Can alternate both for severe pain

**2. Topical:**
- Clove oil on cotton ball, apply to tooth (natural anesthetic)
- Orajel (benzocaine gel)

**3. Saltwater Rinse:**
- 1/2 tsp salt in 8 oz warm water
- Swish 30 seconds, 4-6x daily
- Reduces inflammation

**4. Avoid:**
- Extremely hot/cold foods
- Chewing on affected side
- Sweet/acidic foods

**Abscess (Pus at Gum Line):**
- Needs antibiotics (amoxicillin, clindamycin)
- Warm saltwater rinses
- If abscess visible lump: May drain on own, or needs dentist
- Can be life-threatening if spreads to jaw/neck

---

### Knocked-Out Tooth

**If Permanent Tooth:**

**Immediate Actions (First 5 Minutes Critical):**
1. **Find tooth**
2. **Handle by crown only** (don't touch root)
3. **Rinse gently** if dirty (don't scrub)
4. **Reinsert into socket** if possible (bite down on gauze)
5. **If can't reinsert:** Keep in milk or saliva (not water)
6. **See dentist within 30 minutes** (best chance of saving)

**If Dentist Not Available:**
- Keep clean
- Antibiotics (prevent infection)
- Soft diet
- May need permanent extraction later

**If Baby Tooth:** Don't reinsert (can damage adult tooth below)

---

### Broken/Cracked Tooth

**Treatment:**
1. **Rinse mouth** with warm water
2. **If bleeding:** Gauze 10 minutes
3. **Cold compress** on face (reduce swelling)
4. **Pain control:** Ibuprofen
5. **Temporary filling:** Dental wax or sugar-free gum over sharp edge
6. **Soft diet**

**Dental Filling Kit (Hardware Store or Amazon):**
- Temporary filling material (Dentemp)
- Can last weeks to months
- Follow package directions

## Emergency Childbirth

**If Birth is Imminent and No Medical Help:**

### Signs Labor is Progressing Rapidly

- Contractions 2-3 minutes apart, lasting 60+ seconds
- Urge to push
- Water breaks with bloody show
- Baby's head visible (crowning)

### Supplies (Gather Quickly)

- Clean towels/blankets (many)
- Sterile gloves (or very clean hands)
- Bulb syringe (clear baby's mouth/nose)
- Shoelaces or string (tie umbilical cord)
- Scissors (sterilize in boiling water)
- Plastic sheet/shower curtain (under mother)

### Delivery Steps

**1. Prepare:**
- Wash hands thoroughly (3 minutes, soap)
- Mother in comfortable position (semi-reclined, knees bent)
- Clean area as much as possible

**2. As Head Emerges:**
- Support head gently (don't pull)
- If sac (membrane) covers baby: Break and peel away
- Check for cord around neck: If present, gently slip over head

**3. Body Delivery:**
- Head rotates naturally
- Support head and shoulders as baby slides out (happens quickly after head)
- Baby will be slippery - firm but gentle grip

**4. Immediate Care (First 30 Seconds):**
- Hold baby level with mother (don't lift high or dangle low)
- Wipe mouth and nose with clean cloth
- Bulb syringe: Suction mouth first, then nose
- Dry baby vigorously with towel (stimulates breathing)

**5. If Baby Doesn't Cry/Breathe:**
- Flick soles of feet
- Rub back vigorously
- If still not breathing: Infant CPR (see MEDICAL_FIRST_AID.md)

**6. Warmth (Critical):**
- Dry baby completely
- Place skin-to-skin on mother's chest
- Cover both with blankets
- Baby can't regulate temperature - hypothermia is danger

**7. Umbilical Cord:**
- **Don't cut immediately** (wait 1-5 minutes, let it stop pulsing)
- Tie firmly with shoelace/string at 6 inches from baby
- Tie second tie 2 inches further from baby
- Cut between the two ties with sterilized scissors
- If no scissors: Leave cord attached (will dry and fall off)

**8. Placenta:**
- Delivers 5-30 minutes after baby
- Don't pull on cord (let placenta deliver naturally)
- Gentle push from mother with contraction
- Save placenta (doctor will inspect for completeness)

**9. After Placenta:**
- Massage mother's abdomen (helps uterus contract, stops bleeding)
- Firm massage just below belly button
- Moderate bleeding is normal (soaking 1 pad/hour for first few hours)

### Danger Signs

**Severe Bleeding (Mother):**
- Soaking through pad in <15 minutes
- Large clots
- Dizziness, pale, weak
- **Action:** Massage uterus firmly, elevate legs, keep warm, get help ASAP

**Baby Not Breathing:**
- Blue color
- Limp
- **Action:** Vigorous rubbing, flick feet, infant CPR if needed

**Cord Around Neck (Tight):**
- Can't slip over head
- **Action:** Clamp cord in two places (or tie tightly), cut between clamps

## Improvised Medical Supplies

### When Supplies Run Out

**Bandages:**
- Clean t-shirts (cut into strips)
- Sheets (boiled and dried)
- Menstrual pads (sterile, absorbent - excellent for large wounds)
- Tampons (gunshot/puncture wounds - stops bleeding, but must be removed and cleaned properly)

**Tape:**
- Duct tape (over gauze, not on skin)
- Superglue (wound closure)
- Butterfly strips: Cut tape into thin strips

**Gloves:**
- Plastic bags over hands (not ideal, but barrier)
- Wash hands thoroughly before/after

**Antiseptic:**
- Honey (raw, unpasteurized) - natural antibiotic
- Alcohol (drinking liquor 80+ proof)
- Betadine/iodine (water purification tablets diluted)
- Boiled water (cooled)

**Irrigation:**
- Squeeze bottle
- Clean plastic bag with pinhole
- Syringe without needle (oral medication syringe)

**Splints:**
- Sticks, branches
- Rolled newspaper/magazine
- Cardboard
- Foam sleeping pad

**Ice Pack:**
- Frozen vegetables
- Bag of ice/snow
- Cold water-soaked cloth

**Hot Pack:**
- Rice/beans in sock, microwaved (or heated by fire)
- Hot water bottle
- Warm wet towel

**Sling:**
- Triangular bandage
- T-shirt
- Belt

**Stretcher:**
- Blanket with poles through edges
- Sturdy door/board
- Two backpacks with poles

## First 24 Hours

**Priorities After Major Injury/Illness:**

1. **Stabilize ABCs** (Airway, Breathing, Circulation)
2. **Control bleeding**
3. **Treat for shock**
4. **Immobilize fractures**
5. **Clean wounds** (within 6-8 hours ideal)
6. **Document** (vital signs, injuries, treatments)
7. **Decide:** Evacuate vs. care in place
8. **Hydration/nutrition** (if conscious and no abdominal injury)
9. **Pain management**
10. **Monitor closely** (check vitals every 2-4 hours)

## Days 2-7: Extended Care

**Goals:**
- Prevent infection
- Promote healing
- Maintain nutrition/hydration
- Monitor for complications

**Daily Routine:**
1. **Check vitals** 2-4x daily
2. **Wound care:**
   - Remove bandage
   - Inspect for infection signs
   - Clean/irrigate
   - Fresh bandage
3. **Pain management** (scheduled, not just as needed)
4. **Hydration:** 8-12 cups daily minimum
5. **Nutrition:** High protein, calories for healing
6. **Mobility:** Gentle movement if safe (prevents blood clots, pneumonia)
7. **Document** all changes

**Red Flags:**
- Worsening pain
- New fever
- Increased swelling/redness
- Confusion, disorientation
- Shortness of breath
- → Reevaluate evacuation

---

## Week 2+: Long-Term Care

**Recovery Phase:**
- Most acute danger passed
- Focus on healing, rehabilitation
- Continue monitoring for complications

**Physical Therapy:**
- Gentle range of motion
- Prevent stiffness, muscle loss
- Gradual increase in activity

**Nutrition:**
- High protein (healing)
- Calories (energy for recovery)
- Vitamins (C for wound healing, D for bones)

**Mental Health:**
- Stress of injury + isolation
- Talk through experience
- Maintain routines
- Set small goals

## Medical Stockpile Priorities

**For extended care scenarios, prioritize:**

1. **Antibiotics** (various types - see MEDICAL_MEDICATIONS.md)
2. **Pain management** (ibuprofen, acetaminophen, stronger if possible)
3. **Wound care** (gauze, tape, antibiotic ointment, irrigation supplies)
4. **Suture kit** (if trained or willing to learn)
5. **Splinting materials**
6. **Diagnostic tools** (thermometer, BP cuff, stethoscope)
7. **Reference books** (printed, not digital)
