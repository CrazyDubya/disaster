## Material Selection

### Natural Materials (Free, Abundant)

**Wood:**
- **Logs:** Structural (walls, frame)
- **Poles:** Smaller supports (rafters, purlins)
- **Branches:** Thatching, wattle-and-daub
- **Bark:** Roofing, siding

**Best Trees:**
- Pine, fir, spruce (straight, tall, softwood - easier to work)
- Cedar, juniper (rot-resistant, lightweight)
- Oak, ash (hardwood - strong, durable, heavy)

**Harvest:**
- Dead standing (already dry, but check for rot)
- Live trees (stronger, must dry/season 6-12 months)
- Diameter: 6-12" for logs, 2-6" for poles

**Earth:**
- **Clay:** Mortar, cob, daub, bricks
- **Sand:** Mixing, drainage
- **Soil:** Earthbags, sod roofs, rammed earth
- **Stone:** Foundation, walls, fireplace

**Vegetation:**
- **Grass:** Thatching, bedding, insulation
- **Leaves:** Insulation (2-3 ft thick)
- **Moss:** Chinking (fill gaps), insulation
- **Reeds/cattails:** Thatching, matting

### Scavenged Materials

**Urban/Suburban:**
- Pallets (walls, flooring): Free
- Corrugated metal (roofing): Salvage
- Plywood/OSB: Construction sites (ask permission)
- Tarps, plastic sheeting: Waterproofing
- Insulation batts: Demolition sites
- Windows, doors: Habitat ReStore ($5-50)

**Rural:**
- Barn wood: Siding, flooring
- Fence posts: Framing
- Tin roofing: Waterproof roof

## Foundation Basics

**Why Foundation Matters:**
- Keeps structure off damp ground (rot prevention)
- Level base (structural integrity)
- Protects from moisture, pests, frost heave

### Types

**1. Post Foundation (Simplest):**
- 4-6 posts set in ground
- 2-3 ft deep (below frost line if cold climate)
- Gravel in hole (drainage)
- Tamp firmly
- Posts support floor beams

**Cost:** $50-150
**Time:** 4-8 hours
**Best for:** Cabins, pole barns

**2. Pier Foundation (More Stable):**
- Concrete blocks or poured piers at corners and 6-8 ft intervals
- Set on gravel (drainage)
- Level with shims
- Beams span piers

**Cost:** $100-300
**Time:** 1-2 days
**Best for:** Larger cabins, permanent structures

**3. Rubble Trench (Stone Foundation):**
- Dig trench 12-18" deep, 12-18" wide
- Fill with stone/gravel
- Level top with flat stones or concrete
- Timbers rest on stones

**Cost:** $50-200 (if stone available)
**Time:** 2-4 days
**Best for:** Log cabins, stone structures

**4. Slab (Requires Concrete):**
- Excavate and level
- Gravel base (4-6")
- Rebar grid
- Pour concrete (4-6" thick)

**Cost:** $500-2,000+
**Time:** 1 week (including curing)
**Best for:** Permanent homes (not primitive)

## Beginner Builds

### Lean-To Shelter (Upgraded)

**Size:** 8 ft wide × 6 ft deep × 6 ft tall (1-2 people)

**Materials:**
- 2 posts (6 ft tall, 4-6" diameter): Ridge support
- 1 ridge pole (10 ft long, 4" diameter)
- 10-15 rafters (8 ft long, 2-3" diameter)
- Thatching: Branches, grass, bark, or tarp
- Cordage or nails

**Steps:**
1. Set 2 posts 8 ft apart, 6 ft tall
2. Lash ridge pole across top of posts
3. Lean rafters against ridge pole (angle 45-60 degrees)
4. Space rafters 6-12" apart
5. Weave horizontal branches between rafters (lattice)
6. Thatch from bottom up (overlap like shingles)
7. Thick layer 6-12" (waterproof)

**Time:** 1-2 days
**Cost:** $0-50 (if buying tarp/cordage)
**Shelter life:** Months to 1-2 years

---

### Tarp A-Frame (Semi-Permanent)

**Size:** 10 ft wide × 8 ft deep × 6 ft tall

**Materials:**
- 2 tarps (10×12 ft): Roof and floor
- Ridge pole: 12 ft
- 2 end posts: 6 ft
- Paracord
- Stakes

**Steps:**
1. Set posts 10 ft apart, 6 ft tall
2. Ridge pole across tops
3. Drape tarp over ridge (A-frame shape)
4. Stake out corners
5. Second tarp on ground (floor)
6. Optional: Close ends with debris/tarp

**Time:** 2-4 hours
**Cost:** $50-100
**Shelter life:** 6 months to 2 years (tarp degrades)

## Intermediate Builds

### A-Frame Cabin

**Size:** 8 ft wide × 12 ft long × 8 ft tall (peak)

**Materials:**
- Lumber (if available) OR logs
- Roof material (shingles, metal, thatch)
- Door, window (optional)
- Nails, screws

**Frame:**
1. **Floor:** 2×6 joists on piers, plywood decking
2. **Walls:** 2×4 A-frame trusses every 24" (7 total)
   - Each truss: Two 10 ft boards meet at peak (60-degree angle)
3. **Ridge:** 2×6 board along peak
4. **Purlins:** Horizontal boards across trusses (roof support)
5. **End walls:** Triangular, one with door

**Roof:**
- Plywood sheathing
- Tar paper
- Shingles OR metal OR thick thatch

**Time:** 1-2 weeks (with help)
**Cost:** $500-1,500 (lumber), $0-300 (logs)
**Shelter life:** 10-30 years

**Advantages:**
- Simple design (no complex joints)
- Sheds snow/rain well (steep sides)
- Minimal lumber
- Strong structure

**Downsides:**
- Limited headroom (sloped walls)
- Less usable space

### Dugout / Earth Shelter

**Concept:** Partial underground, earth-covered roof

**Advantages:**
- Insulated (earth = 50-60°F year-round)
- Hidden (low profile)
- Fireproof
- Quiet

**Challenges:**
- Labor-intensive (digging)
- Moisture/drainage critical
- Requires strong roof (earth is heavy)

**Basic Design:**

**Step 1: Site Selection**
- Hillside (easier excavation)
- Well-drained soil (not clay that holds water)
- South-facing (sunlight into entrance)

**Step 2: Excavation**
- Dig into hillside 8-10 ft deep, 10-12 ft wide, 12-14 ft back
- Slope floor toward entrance (drainage)

**Step 3: Walls**
- Timber supports (6×6 posts, logs)
- Stacked stone (dry stack or mortar)
- Earth walls (stable soil only, rib with timbers)

**Step 4: Roof**
- **Critical:** Must support 300+ lbs per sq ft (earth weight)
- Log beams (8-12" diameter) every 2-3 ft
- Crossbeams perpendicular
- Plywood or planks (deck)
- Waterproof layer (plastic sheeting, pond liner)
- 12-18" earth on top
- Sod/grass cover (erosion control)

**Step 5: Entrance**
- Timber framed
- Door at angle (shed water)
- Drainage ditch around entrance

**Step 6: Ventilation**
- Pipe through roof (stove pipe or PVC)
- Intake near door, exhaust high on back wall

**Time:** 2-4 weeks (digging by hand)
**Cost:** $200-800 (timbers, waterproofing)
**Shelter life:** 10-50 years (if well-built)

**Drainage is CRITICAL:**
- French drain around uphill side (gravel-filled trench)
- Ditches divert water away
- Floor slopes out
- Check after rains (fix leaks immediately)

### Wattle-and-Daub Hut

**Concept:** Woven branch walls (wattle) plastered with mud/clay (daub)

**Size:** 10 ft diameter round hut, 8 ft tall

**Materials:**
- Posts (4-6" diameter): 12-20, 8 ft tall
- Wattle (flexible branches - willow, hazel): Hundreds
- Daub: Clay soil, sand, straw, water
- Roof poles
- Thatching (grass, reeds)

**Steps:**

**Frame:**
1. Mark circle (10 ft diameter)
2. Set posts every 2-3 ft around circle, 2 ft deep
3. Top plate: Horizontal pole lashed around top of posts (creates ring)

**Walls (Wattle):**
1. Weave flexible branches horizontally between posts (basket weave)
2. Start at bottom, work up
3. Tight weave (daub base)

**Walls (Daub):**
1. Mix: 3 parts clay soil, 2 parts sand, 1 part straw/grass, water to thick paste
2. Apply to both sides of wattle (press through gaps)
3. Build up 2-3 coats (each 1-2" thick)
4. Smooth surface
5. Let dry (1-2 weeks per coat)

**Roof:**
1. Center pole (10 ft tall)
2. Rafters from top plate to center pole (cone shape)
3. Horizontal lashing around rafters (support thatch)
4. Thatch thick (12-18") from bottom up

**Door:** Frame opening, hang door or hides

**Time:** 3-6 weeks
**Cost:** $0-100
**Shelter life:** 5-20 years (needs daub repairs)

## Advanced Builds

### Log Cabin Basics

**Size:** 12 ft × 16 ft, 8 ft walls

**Logs:** 40-60 logs (8-12" diameter, 12-16 ft long)

**Notching (Corner Joints):**
- **Saddle notch:** Round notch fits over log below (strongest, traditional)
- **Dovetail:** Wedge-shaped notch (very strong, complex)
- **Butt-and-pass:** Simple (logs alternate, rest on each other)

**Steps:**

**Foundation:**
- Pier or stone foundation
- Sill logs (first layer) on foundation, squared and level

**Walls:**
1. Lay first logs on sills (parallel, 12 ft apart)
2. Cut notches on ends (practice on scrap first)
3. Lay perpendicular logs (16 ft, notch over first)
4. Continue stacking, alternating direction
5. Check level frequently
6. Leave openings for door/windows (frame after)

**Chinking (Fill Gaps):**
- Moss, oakum, or modern chinking between logs (seal drafts)

**Roof:**
- Gable roof (A-shape)
- Rafters every 2 ft
- Ridge beam at peak
- Purlins across rafters
- Roofing (shingles, metal, shakes)

**Floor:**
- Joists across foundation
- Plank or plywood decking

**Time:** 3-6 months (solo), 1-2 months (crew)
**Cost:** $500-3,000 (tools, roof, windows, door)
**Shelter life:** 50-100+ years

**Learning Curve:** High (practice notching)

## Roofing Techniques

### Thatching (Natural, Free)

**Materials:** Grass, reeds, cattails, palm fronds, straw

**Steps:**
1. Horizontal supports (rafters + purlins)
2. Bundle thatch (6-12" diameter bundles)
3. Start at bottom edge
4. Tie bundles to purlins (overlap upward)
5. Work up to peak (shingle pattern)
6. Thick layer 12-18" (waterproof)
7. Peak cap (bundles straddling ridge, tied down)

**Pros:** Free, insulates, lasts 10-20 years (if done right), beautiful
**Cons:** Labor-intensive, fire risk, needs steep pitch (45+ degrees)

---

### Wood Shingles/Shakes

**Material:** Split cedar, pine (rot-resistant)

**Steps:**
1. Sheathing (plywood or skip-boards)
2. Tar paper (optional, helps)
3. Shingles from bottom up
4. Overlap 1/3 to 1/2 (2-3 layers thick at any point)
5. Stagger joints (water can't run straight down)
6. Nail twice per shingle

**Pros:** Durable (20-40 years), attractive, insulating
**Cons:** Labor, requires nails, fire risk

---

### Metal Roofing

**Material:** Corrugated steel/aluminum (scavenged or bought)

**Steps:**
1. Rafters/purlins (metal spans 3-4 ft)
2. Lay sheets perpendicular to ridge (overlap 6")
3. Screw down (roofing screws with washers)
4. Overlap sheets at ridge, seal

**Pros:** Long-lasting (30-50 years), waterproof, fire-resistant, lightweight
**Cons:** Expensive (if buying new), noisy in rain, hot in sun

**Scavenging:** Old barns, sheds (free)

## Insulation Methods

**Heat Loss Without Insulation:**
- Freezing in winter, hot in summer
- Fuel waste (if heating)

### Natural Insulation

**Dried Grass/Hay:**
- Pack in walls (if framed), under roof
- 6-12" thick
- Fire risk, rodent habitat

**Leaves:**
- Fill cavities 12-24"
- Cheap, available
- Compresses over time (refill)

**Moss:**
- Chinking between logs
- Moderately insulating

**Earth:**
- Earth-covered roofs (dugout)
- Massive insulation (12-18" = R-20)

**Straw Bales:**
- Stack like bricks
- Plaster both sides (earth or lime)
- Excellent insulation (R-30 to R-40)
- Fireproof (if plastered), cheap

### Scavenged Insulation

**Fiberglass Batts:**
- R-13 to R-30
- Salvage from construction/demo
- Install in framed walls

**Rigid Foam:**
- R-5 to R-10 per inch
- Scavenge or buy
- Walls, under roof

## Heating & Cooking

### Rocket Mass Heater

**Concept:** Super-efficient wood stove, stores heat in mass

**How It Works:**
- Burn small sticks in insulated chamber (super hot, clean burn)
- Exhaust through horizontal pipe embedded in bench (cob, brick)
- Mass absorbs heat, radiates for hours
- Uses 1/10th wood of regular stove

**DIY Build:**
- Barrels, stovepipe, cob/bricks
- Plans online (free)
- $100-300 materials
- 1-2 weeks to build

**Benefits:**
- Minimal wood (twigs, scraps)
- Radiant heat (comfortable)
- Bench (sit on warm mass)
- Can integrate oven

---

### Clay/Cob Oven

**Outdoor oven for baking:**
1. Stone base (3 ft tall)
2. Dome of cob (clay, sand, straw)
3. Door opening
4. Chimney hole at back (draws smoke out)
5. Fire inside, heats mass
6. Remove coals, bake in retained heat

**Cost:** $0-50
**Time:** 2-3 days
**Use:** Bread, pizza, roasting

## Water Collection & Storage

**Roof Catchment:**
- Gutters (bamboo, split logs, metal)
- Downspout to barrel/tank

**Storage:**

## Location-Specific Tips

**Urban/Suburban:**
- Permit requirements (legal builds)
- Setback rules (distance from property line)
- HOA restrictions
- Focus: Workshop, shed, greenhouse (less regulated)

**Rural:**
- More freedom (still check codes)
- Larger builds possible
- Off-grid systems (solar, well)

**Wilderness:**
- No permits (public land = illegal)
- Private land: Get permission
- Leave No Trace if temporary

**Mountain:**
- Extreme weather (snow load, wind)
- Steep roof (snow shed)
- Insulation critical
- Shorter building season

## Safety Considerations

**While Building:**
- Eye protection (sawdust, chips)
- Gloves (splinters, blisters)
- Boots (foot protection)
- Ladder safety (stable base, 3 points of contact)
- Chainsaws (chaps, training)
- Axe (sharp = safer, controlled swings, clear area)

**Fire:**
- Wood stove: Clearance (36" from walls), chimney (proper draft, no creosote buildup)
- Smoke detector, fire extinguisher
- Escape plan (2 exits)

**Structural:**
- Roof load: Snow, thatch (heavy)
- Overbuilt better than underbuilt
- Tie-downs (wind)
- Foundation solid (frost heave, erosion)

**Moisture:**
- Ventilation (prevents mold, rot)
- Drainage (away from foundation)
- Vapor barrier (if insulated)
