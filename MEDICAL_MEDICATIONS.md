## Working with Your Doctor

### How to Request Extra Prescriptions

**Honest Approaches:**

**1. Travel Excuse:**
- "I'm traveling internationally for 3 months"
- "I need a 90-day supply instead of 30-day"
- Most doctors will accommodate

**2. Disaster Preparedness:**
- "I live in a rural area and want extra in case of weather emergencies"
- "After [recent disaster], I want to be better prepared"
- Many doctors supportive, especially post-COVID

**3. Insurance Workaround:**
- Ask doctor to prescribe 2x dose, split pills yourself
- Example: 40mg tablets, cut in half for 20mg dose = double supply
- Works for scored tablets only

**4. 90-Day Fills:**
- Switch to mail-order pharmacy (often required for 90-day)
- Costs less, gets 3x supply
- Refill at day 60 = build up extra month

**5. Pay Cash:**
- For cheap medications, skip insurance
- GoodRx, RxSaver apps (coupons, often cheaper than insurance)
- Pharmacist can't refuse valid prescription

**What NOT to Do:**
- Lie about losing medications repeatedly (red flag)
- Exaggerate symptoms to get more (medical record impact)
- Doctor shop (tracked in prescription databases)

## Antibiotics (Priority 1)

**Why Critical:**
- Treat bacterial infections (life-saving)
- No natural alternatives for serious infections
- Require prescription
- Limited shelf life (but usable past expiration)

### Top 5 Antibiotics to Stockpile

**1. Amoxicillin (500mg)**
- **Treats:** Ear infections, sinus infections, strep throat, UTIs, some pneumonia
- **Supply:** 90-120 capsules (3-4 courses)
- **Dosing:** 500mg 3x daily for 7-10 days
- **Cost:** $4-20 per course (generic)
- **Shelf life:** 2-3 years (longer if refrigerated)
- **Most versatile, first-line for many infections**

**2. Amoxicillin-Clavulanate / Augmentin (875mg/125mg)**
- **Treats:** Resistant infections, bite wounds, sinus infections, pneumonia
- **Supply:** 40-60 tablets (2-3 courses)
- **Dosing:** 875/125mg twice daily for 7-10 days
- **Cost:** $20-40 per course
- **Shelf life:** 2 years
- **Stronger than plain amoxicillin (covers more bacteria)**

**3. Azithromycin / Z-Pack (250mg)**
- **Treats:** Pneumonia, bronchitis, sinus infections, STIs
- **Supply:** 12-18 tablets (2-3 Z-packs)
- **Dosing:** 500mg day 1, then 250mg days 2-5
- **Cost:** $10-30 per Z-pack
- **Shelf life:** 2-3 years
- **Convenient (short course), good for respiratory infections**

**4. Ciprofloxacin / Cipro (500mg)**
- **Treats:** UTIs, severe diarrhea, anthrax (bioterrorism), skin infections
- **Supply:** 40-60 tablets (4-6 courses for UTI)
- **Dosing:** 500mg twice daily for 3-7 days (UTI) or 10-14 days (other)
- **Cost:** $10-40 per course
- **Shelf life:** 3-5 years
- **Broad spectrum, long shelf life**

**5. Doxycycline (100mg)**
- **Treats:** Tick-borne diseases (Lyme, Rocky Mountain spotted fever), pneumonia, acne, malaria prevention
- **Supply:** 60-90 capsules (3-4 courses)
- **Dosing:** 100mg twice daily for 7-14 days
- **Cost:** $5-20 per course
- **Shelf life:** 2-3 years
- **Essential for tick-prone areas**

### Additional Antibiotics (If Budget Allows)

**Cephalexin / Keflex (500mg):**
- Skin infections (cellulitis), UTIs
- Alternative to amoxicillin (penicillin allergy)
- 60-90 capsules
- $10-25 per course

**Metronidazole / Flagyl (500mg):**
- Anaerobic infections, dental abscesses, severe diarrhea (C. diff, giardia)
- 40-60 tablets
- $5-15 per course

**Sulfamethoxazole-Trimethoprim / Bactrim (800/160mg):**
- UTIs, MRSA skin infections, pneumonia
- 40-60 tablets
- $5-20 per course

### Antibiotic Dosing Quick Reference

| Antibiotic | Adult Dose | Pediatric Dose (per kg) | Duration |
|------------|------------|-------------------------|----------|
| Amoxicillin | 500mg 3x daily | 25-50mg/kg/day ÷ 3 doses | 7-10 days |
| Augmentin | 875mg 2x daily | 45mg/kg/day ÷ 2 doses | 7-10 days |
| Azithromycin | 500mg day 1, 250mg days 2-5 | 10mg/kg day 1, 5mg/kg days 2-5 | 5 days |
| Ciprofloxacin | 500mg 2x daily | NOT for children <18 | 3-14 days |
| Doxycycline | 100mg 2x daily | 2-4mg/kg/day ÷ 2 doses (age 8+) | 7-14 days |
| Cephalexin | 500mg 4x daily | 25-50mg/kg/day ÷ 4 doses | 7-10 days |

**Weight-based dosing for children requires calculation - keep reference chart**

### How to Get Antibiotics

**1. Regular Doctor:**
- Ask for extra during normal visit
- Explain preparedness concerns
- Success rate: 30-50%

**2. Telemedicine:**
- Urgent care apps (Teladoc, MDLive)
- Request for travel or refill
- $40-80 per visit
- Success rate: Variable

**3. Dental Antibiotics:**
- Dentists prescribe liberally for tooth infections
- Same antibiotics (amoxicillin, etc.)
- Can stockpile extras

**4. Veterinary Antibiotics:**
- See section below
- Controversial but option

**5. International Pharmacies:**
- Mexico, Canada (over-counter or easier prescription)
- Legal gray area (personal import)
- Quality concerns (counterfeit risk)

## Chronic Disease Medications

**If You Take Daily Medications:**

### Life-Critical Medications (Highest Priority)

**Insulin (Diabetics):**
- 3-6 month supply minimum
- Refrigerate (stable 28 days at room temp)
- Rotate stock (use oldest first)
- Backup: NPH and Regular insulin (cheaper, longer shelf life than modern analogs)
- **Without insulin, Type 1 diabetics die in days**

**Heart Medications:**
- Beta-blockers (metoprolol, atenolol)
- ACE inhibitors (lisinopril, enalapril)
- Blood thinners (warfarin, apixaban)
- Nitroglycerin (for angina - expires quickly, rotate)
- 3-6 month supply

**Seizure Medications:**
- Phenytoin (Dilantin), valproic acid, levetiracetam (Keppra)
- **Missing doses can trigger seizures**
- 6-12 month supply if possible

**Thyroid (Levothyroxine):**
- Very stable, long shelf life (5+ years)
- Cheap ($4/month)
- Easy to stockpile year+
- Missing causes fatigue, but not immediately life-threatening

**Asthma Inhalers:**
- Albuterol rescue inhaler: 3-4 extras
- Controller inhalers (Flovent, etc.): 3 month supply
- Nebulizer + albuterol solution (if electricity available)
- **Severe asthma attack without inhaler = life-threatening**

**Blood Pressure:**
- 3-6 month supply
- Monitor BP at home (cuff $25-50)
- Many cheap generics available

**Psychiatric Medications:**
- Antidepressants, antipsychotics, mood stabilizers
- **Abrupt discontinuation dangerous**
- 3-6 month supply minimum
- Taper gradually if must stop (doctor guidance)

### Stockpiling Strategy for Chronic Meds

**1. 90-Day Prescriptions:**
- Switch from 30-day to 90-day (often required for mail-order)
- Saves money and trips

**2. Early Refills:**
- Refill at day 60-75 of 90-day supply
- Accumulates 15-30 day extra per quarter
- Over time builds surplus

**3. Samples:**
- Ask doctor for samples at each visit
- Keep sealed in cool, dark place

**4. Pharmacy Shopping:**
- Prices vary wildly between pharmacies
- Use GoodRx, check Costco (no membership required for pharmacy)
- Example: Metformin $4/month Walmart vs $50/month some chains

**5. Pay Cash for Extras:**
- Second prescription, different pharmacy, pay out-of-pocket
- Insurance won't cover, but doctor can write prescription

**6. Medical Tourism:**
- Medications dramatically cheaper in Mexico, Canada
- 3-6 month personal supply allowed across US border
- Bring prescription, receipts

## Pain Management

### Over-the-Counter (OTC)

**Ibuprofen (Advil, Motrin) 200mg:**
- **Uses:** Pain, inflammation, fever
- **Stockpile:** 500-1,000 tablets
- **Dosing:** 400-600mg (2-3 tablets) every 6 hours
- **Max:** 2,400mg/day
- **Cost:** $10-20 per 500 tablets (Sam's Club, Costco)
- **Shelf life:** 5+ years
- **Best for:** Muscle pain, dental pain, inflammation

**Acetaminophen (Tylenol) 500mg:**
- **Uses:** Pain, fever (NOT inflammation)
- **Stockpile:** 500-1,000 tablets
- **Dosing:** 500-1,000mg every 6 hours
- **Max:** 3,000mg/day (liver damage risk above)
- **Cost:** $10-20 per 500 tablets
- **Shelf life:** 5+ years
- **Best for:** Fever, pain if can't take ibuprofen

**Aspirin 325mg:**
- **Uses:** Pain, fever, heart attack (blood thinner)
- **Stockpile:** 200-500 tablets
- **Dosing:** 325-650mg every 4-6 hours (pain), 81-325mg daily (heart)
- **Cost:** $5-15 per 500 tablets
- **Shelf life:** 5+ years
- **Keep for heart attack:** Chew 325mg immediately if suspected heart attack

**Naproxen (Aleve) 220mg:**
- **Uses:** Pain, inflammation (longer-lasting than ibuprofen)
- **Stockpile:** 200-500 tablets
- **Dosing:** 220-440mg every 8-12 hours
- **Cost:** $15-25 per 500 tablets
- **Shelf life:** 5+ years

**Combining Pain Meds:**
- Ibuprofen + Acetaminophen: Safe, can take together (different mechanisms)
- Alternate every 3 hours for severe pain
- Don't combine ibuprofen + naproxen (both NSAIDs)

### Prescription Pain Medications

**Tramadol:**
- Moderate pain (stronger than OTC, weaker than opioids)
- Less addiction risk than opioids
- Prescription required
- 50-100mg every 6 hours
- 60-90 tablets useful

**Opioids (Codeine, Hydrocodone, Oxycodone):**
- Severe pain (post-surgery, trauma, end-of-life)
- High addiction risk
- Heavily regulated (hard to stockpile)
- If prescribed, keep extras for emergencies
- Naloxone (Narcan) antidote for overdose

### Topical Pain Relief

**Lidocaine patches/cream:**
- Numbing agent
- Muscle pain, neuropathy
- OTC 4%, prescription 5%

**Capsaicin cream:**
- Arthritis, nerve pain
- OTC, cheap

**Menthol/camphor (Biofreeze, Icy Hot):**
- Temporary relief, muscle aches

## OTC for Common Illnesses

### Allergy & Asthma

**Diphenhydramine (Benadryl) 25mg:**
- Allergies, itching, sleep aid, nausea
- Stockpile: 200-500 tablets
- Dosing: 25-50mg every 6 hours
- Cost: $5-15 per 100
- Shelf life: 5+ years
- **Keep for severe allergic reactions (anaphylaxis bridge until EpiPen)**

**Cetirizine (Zyrtec) 10mg:**
- Daily allergies (non-drowsy)
- Stockpile: 200-365 tablets
- Dosing: 10mg once daily
- Cost: $10-25 per 365
- Shelf life: 3-5 years

**Loratadine (Claritin) 10mg:**
- Daily allergies (non-drowsy)
- Similar to Zyrtec
- Often cheaper

**Pseudoephedrine (Sudafed) 30mg:**
- Nasal congestion (decongestant)
- Behind pharmacy counter (ID required)
- Stockpile: 100-200 tablets
- Dosing: 30-60mg every 4-6 hours
- **More effective than phenylephrine (PE) versions on shelves**

**Guaifenesin (Mucinex) 400mg:**
- Expectorant (thins mucus)
- Stockpile: 100-200 tablets
- Dosing: 400mg every 4 hours
- Drink lots of water with it

### Gastrointestinal

**Loperamide (Imodium) 2mg:**
- Anti-diarrheal
- Stockpile: 100-200 tablets
- Dosing: 4mg initial, 2mg after each loose stool (max 16mg/day)
- Cost: $8-15 per 100
- Shelf life: 5+ years
- **Essential for diarrhea emergencies**

**Bismuth Subsalicylate (Pepto-Bismol):**
- Diarrhea, nausea, upset stomach
- Tablets better for storage than liquid
- Stockpile: 100-200 tablets
- Turns stool black (harmless)

**Omeprazole (Prilosec) 20mg:**
- Heartburn, acid reflux, ulcers
- Now OTC (was prescription)
- Stockpile: 50-100 tablets
- Dosing: 20mg once daily
- Cost: $10-20 per 42

**Famotidine (Pepcid) 20mg:**
- Heartburn (faster-acting than omeprazole)
- Stockpile: 100-200 tablets
- Dosing: 20mg twice daily
- Cost: $10-20 per 100

**Calcium Carbonate (Tums):**
- Quick heartburn relief, calcium supplement
- Cheap, long shelf life
- Multiple bottles

**Docusate (Colace) 100mg:**
- Stool softener (constipation, post-surgery)
- Stockpile: 100-200 capsules
- Gentle, safe

**Sennosides (Senna, Ex-Lax):**
- Laxative (stronger than stool softener)
- Use sparingly (dependency risk)
- Stockpile: 50-100 tablets

### Cough & Cold

**Dextromethorphan (Robitussin DM) 15mg:**
- Cough suppressant
- Stockpile: Tablets or syrup (tablets last longer)
- Dosing: 15-30mg every 6-8 hours
- Shelf life: Syrup 2-3 years, tablets 5+ years

**Phenylephrine (PE):**
- Decongestant (MUCH less effective than pseudoephedrine)
- Not recommended - use pseudoephedrine instead

**Zinc lozenges:**
- Reduces cold duration if taken early
- Stockpile: 3-4 bags
- Start within 24 hours of symptoms

**Vitamin C:**
- May reduce cold severity/duration (debated)
- Cheap, long shelf life
- 500-1,000mg daily

### Sleep & Anxiety

**Diphenhydramine (Benadryl) 25mg:**
- Sleep aid (causes drowsiness)
- Also for allergies
- See above

**Melatonin 3-10mg:**
- Natural sleep hormone
- Jet lag, insomnia
- Stockpile: 100-200 tablets
- Start with 3mg
- Very safe

**Doxylamine (Unisom) 25mg:**
- Sleep aid (stronger than Benadryl for some)
- Stockpile: 50-100 tablets

## Topical Medications

**Antibiotic Ointment:**
- Triple antibiotic (Neosporin): Bacitracin + neomycin + polymyxin
- Bacitracin (single antibiotic - less allergy risk)
- Stockpile: 10-20 tubes
- Shelf life: 3-5 years (use if not discolored/separated)

**Hydrocortisone 1% Cream:**
- Anti-itch, rashes, eczema
- Stockpile: 5-10 tubes
- Shelf life: 3 years

**Clotrimazole 1% (Lotrimin):**
- Antifungal (athlete's foot, jock itch, yeast)
- Stockpile: 3-5 tubes
- Shelf life: 3 years

**Burn Gel / Aloe Vera:**
- Burns, sunburn
- Stockpile: 3-5 tubes/bottles
- Aloe: Refrigerated lasts longer

**Benzocaine (Orajel):**
- Topical numbing (toothache, canker sores)
- Stockpile: 2-3 tubes

**Witch Hazel:**
- Hemorrhoids, minor skin irritations
- Stockpile: 2-3 bottles
- Long shelf life

## Storage & Expiration

### Proper Storage Conditions

**Temperature:**
- Ideal: 68-77°F (20-25°C)
- Acceptable: 59-86°F (15-30°C)
- **Avoid:** Bathrooms (humidity), cars (heat), freezer (some degrade)

**Light:**
- Keep in original containers (light-protected)
- Dark closet or drawer
- Avoid clear containers

**Moisture:**
- Low humidity critical
- Silica gel packets in storage bins
- Avoid bathrooms

**Container:**
- Original bottle best (labeled, light-protected)
- If repackaging: Label clearly with name, dose, expiration
- Vacuum seal for long-term

### Medication Expiration Dates

**The Truth About Expiration:**
- Expiration = Guaranteed potency by manufacturer
- Most medications remain effective years past expiration
- Potency gradually declines (5-10% per year typically)
- Safety: Most remain safe (rare exceptions)

**Study Results:**
- US military tested 100+ medications stored properly
- 90% retained potency 5+ years past expiration
- Some effective 10-15 years later

**Exceptions (Don't Use Past Expiration):**
- Liquid antibiotics (amoxicillin suspension)
- Tetracycline antibiotics (can become toxic - rare)
- Insulin (loses potency, critical dosing)
- Nitroglycerin (loses potency quickly, life-saving drug)
- Epinephrine (EpiPen) - loses potency

**Safe to Use Past Expiration (if stored properly):**
- Tablets/capsules: Most antibiotics, pain meds, etc.
- Ointments/creams (if not discolored or separated)

**How to Assess:**
- Check appearance (discolored, crumbling = discard)
- Smell (foul odor = discard)
- Clumping (moisture exposure = discard)
- If looks/smells normal and stored properly = likely OK to use

**Rotation Strategy:**
- Use FIFO (first in, first out)
- Replace medications 1-2 years before expiration
- Stockpile becomes your regular supply (rotate through)

## Alternative Sources

### Fish Antibiotics (Controversy)

**What Are They:**
- Antibiotics sold for fish tank use (aquarium stores, Amazon)
- Same chemical as human antibiotics (often same manufacturer)
- No prescription required

**Common Fish Antibiotics:**
- Fish Mox = Amoxicillin 250mg
- Fish Mox Forte = Amoxicillin 500mg
- Fish Flex = Cephalexin 250mg or 500mg
- Fish Flox = Ciprofloxacin 500mg
- Fish Pen = Penicillin
- Fish Zole = Metronidazole

**Legal Status:**
- Legal to purchase
- NOT FDA-approved for human use
- Labeled "not for human consumption"

**Quality Concerns:**
- Same manufacturers as human versions (often)
- No FDA oversight for fish versions
- Purity/dosing not guaranteed
- Counterfeit risk from sketchy sellers

**Safety Considerations:**
- **Risk:** Taking wrong antibiotic (incorrect diagnosis)
- **Risk:** Allergic reactions
- **Risk:** Contributing to antibiotic resistance
- **Risk:** Quality/contamination

**When Considered:**
- True emergency (no medical access)
- Knowledge of proper diagnosis and dosing
- Reputable source (Thomas Labs brand most recommended)
- Understand risks

**Ethical/Medical Perspective:**
- Most doctors strongly discourage
- Veterinarians say "not intended for humans"
- Preppers say "better than nothing in SHTF"
- **Best approach: Get human prescriptions properly**

### Veterinary Supply Stores

**Other Vet Meds Sometimes Considered:**
- Antibiotics for livestock (injectable)
- Dosing very different
- Higher contamination risk
- **Not recommended except true survival scenario**

### International Pharmacies

**Mexico:**
- Most antibiotics over-counter
- Walk into pharmacy, buy
- Personal import: 3-month supply allowed
- Quality variable (stick to known chains)
- Cost: 50-90% cheaper than US

**Canada:**
- Online pharmacies ship to US
- Require prescription (more legit)
- Cost: 30-70% cheaper
- Quality generally good

**India/Overseas:**
- Very cheap
- Counterfeit risk HIGH
- Shipping slow/unreliable
- Legal gray area
- Not recommended
