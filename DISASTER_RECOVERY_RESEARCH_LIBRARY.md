## IT Systems & Data Recovery

### NIST Special Publication 800-34
**Title:** Contingency Planning Guide for Federal Information Systems
**Source:** National Institute of Standards and Technology
**Type:** Government Publication (Free PDF)
**URL:** https://csrc.nist.gov/publications/detail/sp/800-34/rev-1/final
**Description:** The primary U.S. government resource providing guidance for IT contingency planning. Includes templates, sample plans, and FAQs for developing effective Information System Contingency Plans (ISCPs).

**Key Features:**
- Contingency planning policy statements
- Business impact analysis guidance
- Preventive controls identification
- Recovery strategies for IT systems
- Aligns with ISO 22301, FFIEC, and NIST SP 800-53

### DRLM (Disaster Recovery Linux Manager)
**Source:** Open Source Project
**Type:** Free/Open Source Software
**URL:** https://drlm.org/
**GitHub:** https://github.com/brainupdaters/drlm
**License:** GPL
**Description:** Central Management Open Source Software for Linux Disaster Recovery and System Migrations, based on Relax-and-Recover (ReaR).

**Key Features:**
- Central management and deployment for Linux DR
- System recovery in minutes
- Vendor-agnostic solution
- System migrations and OS deployments
- Documentation at docs.drlm.org

**Type:** Free PDF Toolkit
**Description:** Guidance for protecting financial documents and assets during disasters.

### Basic Emergency Kit Components (FEMA Recommendations)

**Water:**
- 1 gallon per person per day
- Minimum 3-day supply (72 hours)
- Recommend 2-week supply

**Food:**
- 3-day supply of non-perishable food minimum
- Consider dietary needs and preferences

**Additional Items:**
- Battery-powered or hand crank radio
- NOAA Weather Radio
- Flashlight
- First aid kit
- Extra batteries
- Whistle (to signal for help)
- Dust masks or cloth face coverings
- Soap and hand sanitizer
- Prescription medications
- Non-prescription medications (pain relievers, etc.)
- Infant formula and supplies (if applicable)
- Pet food and supplies (if applicable)
- Cash or traveler's checks
- Important family documents (copies)
- Sleeping bags or warm blankets
- Complete change of clothing
- Fire extinguisher
- Matches in waterproof container

#### FEMA G251 Student Manual
**Title:** Workshop in Emergency Management: Amateur Radio Resources

#### Ham Radio Emergency Frequencies List
**URL:** https://strykerradios.com/ham-radios/ham-radio-emergency-frequencies-common-uses/
**Type:** Free PDF List
**Description:** Popular ham radio emergency frequencies and their uses.

### Key Organizations

**ARES (Amateur Radio Emergency Service)**
- Established: 1935
- Membership: Licensed amateur radio operators
- Function: Volunteer emergency communications
- Registration: Voluntary with local emergency management

**RACES (Radio Amateur Civil Emergency Service)**
- Established: 1952
- Function: Standby Civil Defense radio service
- Integration: Works with government agencies

**AUXCOMM (Auxiliary Communications)**
- Function: Supports emergency communications
- Training: FEMA Independent Study courses (IS-100, IS-700)

### Why HAM Radio?
- Independent of terrestrial infrastructure
- Functions when commercial systems fail
- No dependency on cell towers or internet
- Direct peer-to-peer communication
- Wide area coverage capability

### Training Resources
**FEMA Independent Study Courses:**
- IS-100: Introduction to Incident Command System
- IS-700: National Incident Management System (NIMS)
- Often required for ARES, RACES, AUXCOMM participation

## Usage Guidelines

### How to Use This Library

1. **Assessment Phase:**
   - Identify your specific disaster recovery needs
   - Determine applicable regulatory requirements
   - Assess organizational capabilities and resources

2. **Planning Phase:**
   - Download relevant templates and guides
   - Customize for your specific context
   - Engage stakeholders in planning process
   - Align with industry standards and frameworks

3. **Implementation Phase:**
   - Deploy selected tools and software
   - Train staff and volunteers
   - Document procedures and contacts
   - Establish communication protocols

4. **Testing Phase:**
   - Conduct regular drills and exercises
   - Test backup and recovery systems
   - Validate communication channels
   - Update plans based on lessons learned

5. **Maintenance Phase:**
   - Review and update plans annually
   - Monitor for new threats and hazards
   - Stay informed about new resources
   - Maintain training and certifications

### Best Practices

- **Layer Your Defenses:** Combine multiple approaches (IT systems, physical preparedness, training)
- **Regular Testing:** Test plans and systems regularly, don't wait for actual disasters
- **Community Integration:** Participate in local emergency management programs
- **Documentation:** Keep both digital and physical copies of critical plans
- **Communication:** Establish multiple communication channels and backup methods
- **Training:** Ensure key personnel are trained and certified
- **Updates:** Review and update plans after every drill and actual incident
- **Coordination:** Align with local, state, and federal emergency management frameworks

### Legal and Compliance Considerations

- **HIPAA:** Healthcare organizations must comply with specific security and privacy rules
- **NIST:** Federal agencies and contractors should follow NIST guidelines
- **ISO Standards:** Consider ISO 22301 (Business Continuity) and ISO 27031 (ICT Readiness)
- **Industry-Specific:** Follow industry-specific regulations (financial, education, etc.)
- **Local Requirements:** Check state and local emergency management requirements
