## Why Food Preparedness Matters

**The Reality:**
- Grocery stores stock 3 days of food for local population
- Supply chains break in 24-48 hours during disasters
- Panic buying empties shelves in hours
- Average household has 3-7 days of food
- FEMA recommends minimum 2-week supply
- Long-term disasters require months of self-sufficiency

**What History Teaches:**
- Hurricane Katrina (2005): No food for 5+ days
- COVID-19 (2020): Bare shelves for weeks
- Texas Winter Storm (2021): 4+ days without supplies
- Supply chain disruptions (2021-2023): Ongoing shortages

**You need:** Progressive food storage matching timeline preparedness

## Food Preservation Methods

### Why Preservation Matters

**Long-term storage requires preservation:**
- Fresh food lasts days to weeks
- Preserved food lasts months to decades
- Reduces waste and cost
- Provides variety year-round
- Essential skill for multi-year preparedness

---

### Method 1: Canning (USDA Guidelines)

**What is Canning:**
- Heat processing in sealed jars
- Kills bacteria/enzymes
- Creates vacuum seal
- Shelf life: 1-5 years properly stored

**Two Methods:**

**Water Bath Canning (High-Acid Foods):**
- Fruits (pH < 4.6)
- Jams and jellies
- Pickles
- Tomatoes (with added acid)
- Equipment: Large pot, jars, lids, rings
- Cost: $50-100 startup

**Pressure Canning (Low-Acid Foods):**
- Vegetables (pH > 4.6)
- Meats
- Soups and stews
- Beans
- Equipment: Pressure canner ($100-300)
- Required for safety (botulism prevention)

**Step-by-Step Water Bath Canning:**

1. **Prepare jars:** Wash in hot soapy water, sterilize in boiling water
2. **Prepare food:** Follow tested recipe exactly
3. **Fill jars:** Leave proper headspace (1/4" to 1/2")
4. **Remove air bubbles:** Use plastic spatula
5. **Wipe rims:** Clean with damp cloth
6. **Apply lids:** Place new lid, screw on ring fingertip-tight
7. **Process:** Submerge in boiling water, process for recipe time
8. **Cool:** Remove, let sit undisturbed 12-24 hours
9. **Check seals:** Lid should not flex when pressed
10. **Store:** Remove rings, label with contents and date

**Pressure Canning Steps:**
- Similar to water bath
- Use pressure canner (not pressure cooker)
- Follow pressure and time guidelines exactly
- Adjust for altitude (add pressure/time)
- Never guess - use tested recipes only

**Safety Rules:**
- Always use tested recipes (USDA, Ball, Extension)
- Never reuse lids (rings are reusable)
- Discard unsealed jars (refrigerate and use within week)
- If jar looks/smells bad = THROW OUT
- Botulism is deadly - follow guidelines exactly

**Free USDA Resources:**
- Complete Guide to Home Canning (free PDF)
- URL: https://nchfp.uga.edu/publications/publications_usda.html
- National Center for Home Food Preservation: https://nchfp.uga.edu
- Ball Canning website (free recipes)

**What to Can:**
- Tomato sauce (from garden harvest)
- Fruit preserves
- Pickles (cucumbers, vegetables)
- Salsa
- Applesauce
- Meat (requires pressure canner)
- Bone broth
- Bean soups

**Cost Analysis:**
- Jars (quart): $12-15 per dozen (one-time)
- Lids (regular): $3-5 per dozen (annual)
- Rings: $3-5 per dozen (reusable for years)
- **Per quart canned:** $0.50-1.00 ongoing cost

### Method 2: Dehydrating

**What is Dehydrating:**
- Removes 90-95% water content
- Bacteria/mold can't grow without water
- Shelf life: 1-10 years (depends on food and storage)
- Reduces weight by 75-90%

**Equipment Options:**

**Electric Dehydrator:**
- Cost: $40-300
- Best for: Consistent results, large batches
- Recommended: Excalibur ($200-300), Nesco ($60-100)
- Power use: 300-600 watts

**Oven Dehydrating:**
- Cost: $0 (use existing oven)
- Best for: Small batches, trying dehydrating
- Downsides: Less efficient, harder to control temp
- Set to lowest temp (140-170°F), prop door open

**Sun Drying:**
- Cost: $0-20 (screens)
- Best for: Hot, dry climates (Southwest US)
- Downsides: Weather dependent, slow, pest issues
- Use: Herbs, hot peppers, tomatoes

**Solar Dehydrator (DIY):**
- Cost: $50-150 (build yourself)
- Best for: Off-grid, hot climates
- Free plans available online

**What to Dehydrate:**

**Fruits:**
- Apples (chips, leather): 6-12 hours
- Bananas: 8-12 hours
- Berries: 12-24 hours
- Pineapple: 12-18 hours
- Mango: 12-16 hours
- Peaches: 12-18 hours

**Vegetables:**
- Tomatoes: 8-14 hours
- Zucchini: 8-12 hours
- Mushrooms: 6-10 hours
- Onions: 6-10 hours
- Peppers: 8-12 hours
- Carrots: 8-12 hours
- Corn: 8-12 hours

**Meats (Jerky):**
- Beef: 4-6 hours
- Turkey: 4-6 hours
- Fish: 4-8 hours
- **Safety:** Pre-cook or heat to 160°F

**Herbs:**
- Basil, oregano, thyme: 2-4 hours
- Cilantro, parsley: 2-4 hours

**Step-by-Step Dehydrating:**

1. **Prepare food:**
   - Wash thoroughly
   - Slice uniformly (1/4" thickness ideal)
   - Pre-treat if needed (lemon juice for apples)

2. **Arrange on trays:**
   - Single layer, no overlapping
   - Leave space for air circulation

3. **Set temperature:**
   - Fruits: 135°F
   - Vegetables: 125°F
   - Meats: 160°F (finish at 145°F)
   - Herbs: 95-115°F

4. **Dehydrate:**
   - Check periodically
   - Rotate trays if needed
   - Test for dryness (no moisture when squeezed)

5. **Condition (fruits/vegetables):**
   - Place in jar for 7-10 days
   - Shake daily
   - Redistributes moisture
   - Prevents mold

6. **Package:**
   - Vacuum seal bags (best)
   - Mason jars with oxygen absorbers
   - Mylar bags with oxygen absorbers

7. **Store:**
   - Cool, dark, dry location
   - 50-60°F ideal
   - Check monthly for first 6 months

**Rehydrating:**
- Soak in water 15 minutes to 2 hours
- Use 1:1 or 2:1 water ratio
- Warm water speeds rehydration
- Save soaking water for cooking (nutrients)

**Cost Analysis:**
- Dehydrator: $60-300 (one-time)
- Electricity: $0.03-0.10 per load
- **Per pound dehydrated:** $0.10-0.50 ongoing

### Method 3: Fermenting

**What is Fermenting:**
- Beneficial bacteria preserve food
- Creates probiotics (gut health)
- Increases nutrition and digestibility
- Shelf life: Weeks (refrigerated) to months (cellar)

**Equipment:**
- Mason jars: $1-2 each
- Fermentation weights: $5-10
- Airlock lids (optional): $10-20 for set
- Large bowl/crock (optional): $30-100

**What to Ferment:**

**Vegetables:**
- Sauerkraut (cabbage)
- Kimchi (cabbage, radish, spice)
- Pickles (cucumbers, carrots, etc.)
- Fermented salsa
- Beets
- Green beans

**Dairy (if milk available):**
- Yogurt
- Kefir
- Cheese

**Beverages:**
- Kombucha (requires SCOBY)
- Water kefir
- Ginger beer

**Basic Sauerkraut Recipe:**

**Ingredients:**
- 1 medium cabbage (2 lbs)
- 1-2 tablespoons salt (non-iodized)

**Steps:**
1. Shred cabbage finely
2. Place in bowl, add salt
3. Massage/pound 10 minutes (releases liquid)
4. Pack tightly into quart jar
5. Pour liquid over (should cover cabbage)
6. Weight down (keeps submerged)
7. Cover loosely or use airlock lid
8. Ferment at room temp 3-10 days
9. Taste daily (sour = ready)
10. Refrigerate to slow fermentation

**Safety:**
- Use non-iodized salt (iodine kills bacteria)
- Keep vegetables submerged (mold grows on surface)
- If mold appears: Skim off, cabbage below is fine
- Foul smell = bad (throw out)
- Fizzy/sour smell = good

**Benefits:**
- Probiotics (gut health)
- Increased vitamin C, K
- Preserves harvest
- Requires no electricity
- Adds variety to stored foods

### Method 4: Root Cellaring

**What is Root Cellaring:**
- Cold, humid storage (32-40°F, 90-95% humidity)
- Extends life of fresh produce
- No processing required
- Shelf life: 2-8 months depending on vegetable

**Ideal Root Cellar Conditions:**
- Temperature: 32-40°F
- Humidity: 90-95%
- Ventilation: Moderate air circulation
- Darkness: No light

**Creating Root Cellar:**

**Option 1: Basement Corner:**
- Partition off NE or NW corner
- Insulate walls/ceiling
- Add vent to outside (cool air in)
- Add vent high on wall (warm air out)
- Cost: $100-500

**Option 2: Buried Container:**
- Old freezer or barrel (drainage holes)
- Bury at angle on hillside
- Vent pipe for air
- Insulate lid
- Cost: $50-200

**Option 3: Underground Cellar:**
- Dig 6-8 feet deep
- Concrete or stone walls
- Wooden shelving
- Proper venting
- Cost: $2,000-10,000

**Option 4: Garage/Shed (Cold Climates):**
- Insulated cooler/bin
- Monitor temperature
- Move indoors if too cold
- Cost: $50-200

**What to Store in Root Cellar:**

**Long Storage (4-8 months):**
- Potatoes (dry first, 38-40°F)
- Carrots (in sand, 32-40°F)
- Beets (in sand, 32-40°F)
- Turnips (32-35°F)
- Parsnips (32-35°F)
- Onions (dry, 32-40°F)
- Garlic (dry, 32-40°F)
- Winter squash (dry, 50-55°F)
- Cabbage (32-40°F)

**Medium Storage (2-4 months):**
- Apples (32-40°F, separate from vegetables)
- Pears (32-40°F)
- Sweet potatoes (55-60°F, dry first 2 weeks)

**Short Storage (1-2 months):**
- Tomatoes (green, will ripen)
- Peppers

**Storage Tips:**
- Don't wash before storing (remove excess dirt only)
- Check weekly, remove any rotting items
- Store apples separately (emit ethylene gas)
- Layer root vegetables in damp sand or sawdust
- Hang onions/garlic in mesh bags
- Keep potatoes in dark (light causes green/toxic)

### Method 5: Freezing

**What is Freezing:**
- Stops bacterial growth at 0°F or below
- Retains most nutrition (95%+)
- Shelf life: 6-12 months (quality), indefinite (safety)

**Equipment:**
- Chest freezer: $200-600 (7-25 cu ft)
- Upright freezer: $400-1,000 (better organization)
- Vacuum sealer: $50-200 (extends life)
- Freezer bags/containers: $10-30

**Power Requirements:**
- Chest freezer: 100-250 watts (efficient)
- Upright freezer: 150-300 watts
- Backup power needed (generator, solar)

**What to Freeze:**

**Vegetables (blanch first):**
- Corn, peas, green beans: 8-12 months
- Broccoli, cauliflower: 8-12 months
- Peppers (no blanching needed): 6-8 months
- Tomatoes (for cooking): 8-12 months

**Fruits:**
- Berries: 8-12 months
- Peaches, apples: 8-12 months
- Citrus juice: 8-12 months

**Meats:**
- Beef, pork: 6-12 months
- Poultry: 9-12 months
- Fish: 3-6 months
- Ground meat: 3-4 months

**Prepared Foods:**
- Soups, stews: 2-3 months
- Casseroles: 2-3 months
- Bread, baked goods: 3-6 months

**Blanching Vegetables (before freezing):**
1. Boil large pot of water
2. Prepare ice bath (large bowl)
3. Submerge vegetables in boiling water:
   - Leafy greens: 2 minutes
   - Broccoli: 3 minutes
   - Corn on cob: 7-11 minutes
   - Green beans: 3 minutes
4. Immediately plunge into ice bath (stops cooking)
5. Drain thoroughly
6. Package and freeze immediately

**Packaging:**
- Vacuum seal (best, removes air)
- Freezer bags (squeeze out air)
- Rigid containers (leave headspace for liquids)
- Label with contents and date

**Disaster Considerations:**
- Grid down = lost food in 24-48 hours
- Generator can extend (expensive)
- Solar backup systems ($3,000-10,000)
- Don't rely solely on freezing for long-term prep
- Freezing is supplemental to shelf-stable storage

**Cost Analysis:**

**Cons:**

## Storage Techniques & Locations

### Optimal Storage Conditions

**Temperature:**
- Ideal: 50-60°F
- Acceptable: 40-70°F
- Every 10°F increase cuts shelf life in half
- Avoid: Attics (too hot), unheated garages (freezing)

**Humidity:**
- Ideal: 15% or less for dry goods
- Use: Desiccant packs, oxygen absorbers
- Avoid: Basements (moisture), coastal areas (high humidity)

**Light:**
- Ideal: Complete darkness
- Light degrades vitamins (especially A, C)
- Use: Opaque containers, dark rooms, covered shelves

**Pests:**
- Prevention: Airtight containers, cleanliness
- Use: Food-grade buckets, Mylar bags, bay leaves (natural repellent)
- Check: Monthly for signs of infestation

---

### Long-Term Storage Containers

**5-Gallon Food-Grade Buckets:**
- Capacity: 35-40 lbs (depending on food)
- Cost: $3-7 each
- Source: Home Depot, Lowe's, bakeries (free used)
- Lids: Regular ($1-2) or Gamma Seal ($8-12)
- Use with: Mylar bags + oxygen absorbers

**Mylar Bags + Oxygen Absorbers:**
- Mylar bags (5-gallon): $1.50-3.00 each
- Oxygen absorbers (2000cc): $0.20-0.40 each
- Creates: Hermetic seal, no oxygen (prevents oxidation)
- Shelf life extension: 10-30 years for grains/beans

**How to Package in Buckets:**
1. Place Mylar bag inside bucket
2. Fill with food (leave 2-3" at top)
3. Add oxygen absorber (2000cc for 5-gallon)
4. Seal Mylar with iron (clothes iron or hair straightener)
5. Let sit 24 hours (bag will shrink)
6. Snap on bucket lid
7. Label with contents and date

**Mason Jars + Oxygen Absorbers:**
- Quart jars: $12-15 per dozen
- Oxygen absorbers (300cc): $0.15-0.25 each
- Use for: Smaller quantities, easy access
- Shelf life: 5-10 years

**#10 Cans (Commercial):**
- Large cans (108 oz / 3.2 qt)
- Pre-packaged freeze-dried foods
- Cost: $5-15 per can (empty) for DIY canning
- Shelf life: 25-30 years

## Rotation Systems (FIFO)

### Why Rotation Matters

**Problems with poor rotation:**
- Food expires and wastes money
- Nutritional degradation
- Potential food safety issues
- Inefficient use of storage space

**Benefits of FIFO (First In, First Out):**
- Minimizes waste
- Ensures freshness
- Familiar with stored foods
- Identifies gaps in storage

---

### Setting Up FIFO System

**Step 1: Organize by Category**
- Grains (rice, pasta, oats)
- Legumes (beans, lentils)
- Canned vegetables
- Canned fruits
- Canned proteins
- Condiments & seasonings
- Snacks & comfort foods

**Step 2: Label Everything**
- Purchase date or expiration date
- Use painter's tape or permanent marker
- Label front of container/can

**Step 3: Front-to-Back Shelving**
- Newest items go in back
- Remove items from front
- Slanted shelving helps (cans roll forward)

**Step 4: Create Inventory**
- Spreadsheet or app
- Track: Item, quantity, expiration, location
- Update monthly
- Flag items nearing expiration

**Step 5: Weekly Usage**
- Pull from storage for regular cooking
- Replace immediately after grocery shopping
- Practice cooking with stored foods

## Complete Timeline Summary

### 72 Hours
- **Food:** 3 days ready-to-eat, no cooking required
- **Amount:** 6,000-7,200 calories per person
- **Cost:** $60-120 per person
- **Storage:** Backpack or small tote

### 2 Weeks
- **Food:** Pantry staples, minimal cooking
- **Amount:** 28,000-33,600 calories per person
- **Cost:** $150-350 per person
- **Storage:** Pantry + closet space

### 1 Month
- **Food:** Bulk staples + variety
- **Amount:** 60,000-72,000 calories per person
- **Cost:** $300-1,000 per person
- **Storage:** 8-12 cubic feet per person

### 3-6 Months
- **Food:** Scaled bulk + freeze-dried
- **Amount:** 180,000-540,000 calories per person
- **Cost:** $1,500-6,000 per person
- **Storage:** 40-60 cubic feet per person

### 1 Year+
- **Food:** Mormon plan + variety
- **Amount:** 730,000-876,000 calories per person
- **Cost:** $2,500-12,000 per person
- **Storage:** 100-150 cubic feet per person
