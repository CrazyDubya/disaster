## Why 72 Hours?

The 72-hour kit is **the foundation of all emergency preparedness**. Here's why:

- **Emergency Response Time:** After major disasters, it typically takes 72 hours for organized emergency response to reach most areas
- **Evacuation:** Enough supplies to sustain you if you must leave your home quickly
- **Shelter-in-Place:** Enough supplies to survive at home if utilities are disrupted
- **Universal Need:** Applicable to nearly all emergency scenarios

### Common 72-Hour Emergency Scenarios

- 🌪️ **Natural Disasters:** Hurricanes, tornadoes, earthquakes, floods
- 🔥 **Fires:** Wildfires, house fires requiring evacuation
- ⚡ **Power Outages:** Grid failures, ice storms, heat waves
- 💨 **Severe Weather:** Blizzards, severe storms, extreme temperatures
- 🚨 **Evacuations:** Chemical spills, gas leaks, civil emergencies
- 🌊 **Flooding:** Flash floods, dam failures, coastal surge

## Water - 3 Days

### Minimum Requirement
**1 gallon per person per day = 3 gallons per person for 72 hours**

- ½ gallon for drinking
- ½ gallon for cooking and hygiene

### Storage Options

**Purchased Water (Easiest)**
- Commercial bottled water (check expiration dates)
- Store in cool, dark place
- Rotate every 6-12 months

**DIY Water Storage**
- Food-grade water containers
- Clean 2-liter soda bottles (free!)
- Water bricks or jerry cans
- **Never use milk jugs** (impossible to clean completely)

### Portable Water Sources

**Water purification for on-the-go:**
- Water purification tablets (iodine or chlorine)
- LifeStraw or similar personal filters
- Portable water filter (Sawyer, Katadyn)
- Bleach for purification (8 drops per gallon)

## Food - 3 Days

### Calorie Requirements
- **Adult Male:** ~2,400 calories/day = 7,200 calories total
- **Adult Female:** ~2,000 calories/day = 6,000 calories total
- **Children:** ~1,400-2,200 calories/day depending on age

### Food Selection Criteria

✅ **Must Have:**
- No refrigeration required
- No cooking needed (or minimal)
- Long shelf life (6+ months)
- Compact and lightweight
- Familiar foods (reduce stress)

❌ **Avoid:**
- Highly perishable items
- Foods requiring significant water for preparation
- Foods in glass containers (can break)
- Foods that increase thirst (very salty/spicy)

### Recommended Foods

**Ready-to-Eat Options:**
- Protein/energy bars
- Trail mix, nuts, dried fruit
- Jerky (beef, turkey, salmon)
- Peanut butter or nut butter packets
- Crackers
- Canned fruit, vegetables
- Tuna/chicken/salmon pouches
- Ready-to-eat soup cups
- Granola
- Instant oatmeal packets
- Cereal (can eat dry)

**Comfort Foods (Important for morale!):**
- Hard candy
- Chocolate bars
- Cookies
- Instant coffee/tea

## Shelter & Warmth

### Core Body Temperature is Critical
- Hypothermia can kill in hours
- Hyperthermia (overheating) is equally dangerous
- Shelter is often overlooked in 72-hour kits

### Essential Items

**For Warmth:**
- Emergency blanket (mylar/"space blanket") - reflects 90% body heat
- Warm sleeping bag or wool blankets
- Extra clothing layers
- Hand warmers/heat packs
- Emergency poncho (wind/rain protection)

**For Shelter:**
- Tent or emergency tube tent
- Tarp (8x10 or larger) - incredibly versatile
- Paracord or rope (50-100 feet)
- Duct tape
- Emergency shelter pop-up

**Temperature Regulation:**
- Weather-appropriate clothing
- Rain gear
- Sturdy shoes/boots
- Hat (sun and cold protection)
- Gloves
- Sunglasses

### DIY Emergency Shelter (Tarp Methods)

**A-Frame Shelter:**
1. String rope between two trees (6-8 feet high)
2. Drape tarp over rope
3. Stake corners to ground
4. Can be built in 5-10 minutes

**AMC - How to Build an Emergency Tarp Shelter**

## Sanitation & Hygiene

### Why Sanitation Matters in 72 Hours

Poor sanitation causes disease spread faster than almost any other factor in disasters. Even in 72 hours, basic hygiene is critical.

### Essential Supplies

**Personal Hygiene:**
- Toilet paper
- Feminine hygiene products
- Soap (bar soap lasts longer)
- Hand sanitizer (60%+ alcohol)
- Toothbrush and toothpaste
- Wet wipes/baby wipes
- Towel or washcloth
- Deodorant

**Waste Management:**
- Portable toilet or bucket with liner
- Plastic bags (heavy duty)
- Bleach or disinfectant
- Toilet paper
- Hand sanitizer

### Emergency Toilet Solutions

**Bucket Toilet (Cheapest DIY Option):**
1. 5-gallon bucket with lid
2. Heavy-duty trash bags
3. Kitty litter or sawdust (absorbs liquid/odor)
4. Toilet seat lid (optional, increases comfort)

**Commercial Options:**
- Luggable Loo
- Portable camping toilets
- WAG bags (waste alleviation and gelling)

## Storage & Maintenance

### Where to Store Your 72-Hour Kit

**Primary Location:** Near main exit of home
**Secondary Location:** In vehicle
**Portable:** Use backpacks or rolling suitcases

### Storage Containers

**Good Options:**
- Waterproof backpacks
- Plastic totes with lids
- Duffel bags
- Rolling suitcases
- 5-gallon buckets with gamma lids

**Poor Options:**
- Cardboard boxes (not waterproof)
- Cloth bags (not waterproof)
- Anything too heavy to carry

### Maintenance Schedule

**Every 6 Months:**
- [ ] Check expiration dates on food and water
- [ ] Test batteries and replace if needed
- [ ] Update medications
- [ ] Review and update documents
- [ ] Check condition of clothing (kids grow!)
- [ ] Test flashlights and radios
- [ ] Rotate food and water

**Annually:**
- [ ] Complete inventory review
- [ ] Update emergency contacts
- [ ] Review family emergency plan
- [ ] Practice evacuation drill
- [ ] Rotate all stored items

## Testing Your Kit

### Practice Scenarios

**Once Per Year Minimum:**

1. **Power Outage Drill (12-24 hours)**
   - Turn off main power
   - Use only items from your kit
   - Practice cooking without power
   - Test lighting solutions

2. **Evacuation Drill**
   - Set a timer for 10 minutes
   - Grab your kit and evacuate
   - Drive to designated meeting point
   - Did you forget anything?

3. **Shelter-in-Place Drill (Weekend)**
   - Use only food/water from kit
   - Test sanitation solutions
   - Identify what's missing or inadequate

### Learn From Testing

**After each drill, ask:**
- What worked well?
- What was uncomfortable or difficult?
- What did we forget?
- What needs improvement?
- Are quantities adequate?

**Update your kit based on real experience!**

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can I make one kit for my whole family?**
A: For shelter-in-place, yes. For evacuation, have individual backpacks for each person who can carry one (including kids).

**Q: How heavy should my kit be?**
A: General rule: No more than 20-25% of body weight. A 150 lb person should carry max 30-37 lbs.

**Q: Where's the best place to buy supplies?**
A: Dollar stores, warehouse clubs (Costco, Sam's), camping stores, and online retailers. Don't overlook yard sales for containers.

**Q: Should I buy pre-made kits?**
A: They're convenient but often expensive and may include items you don't need. Building your own is more cost-effective and customized.

**Q: What about firearms for protection?**
A: This is a personal decision based on your comfort level, training, local laws, and situation. If you choose to include firearms, ensure proper training and legal compliance.

**Q: How do I prepare on a tight budget?**
A: Build gradually (one category per month), buy items on sale, use coupons, repurpose containers, share costs with family members.
