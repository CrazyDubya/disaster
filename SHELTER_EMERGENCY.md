## Shelter Priorities (Order of Importance)

**The Rule of Threes:**
- 3 minutes without air
- 3 hours without shelter (extreme weather)
- 3 days without water
- 3 weeks without food

**Shelter is second only to breathing**

### What Shelter Provides

**Protection from:**
1. **Weather** (rain, snow, wind, sun, cold, heat)
2. **Temperature** (hypothermia, hyperthermia)
3. **Elements** (UV, precipitation)
4. **Danger** (animals, people, debris)

**Psychological Benefits:**
- Sense of security
- Rest and recovery
- Privacy
- Morale

## Shelter in Place (At Home)

**Most Common Scenario: Stay at Home During Disaster**

### When to Shelter in Place

- Hurricane (if not evacuation zone)
- Winter storm / blizzard
- Power outage
- Pandemic
- Civil unrest
- Earthquake aftermath
- Tornado (in basement/interior room)
- Chemical spill (seal home)

### Home Shelter Preparation

**Before Disaster:**

**1. Identify Safe Rooms:**
- **Tornado:** Basement or interior room (lowest floor, no windows, center of home)
- **Hurricane:** Interior room away from windows, first floor
- **Earthquake:** Doorway, under sturdy table, away from windows
- **Chemical/biological:** Interior room, upper floor (gases sink), seal windows/doors

**2. Prepare Safe Room:**
- Remove heavy objects from shelves
- Keep flashlights, radio, supplies
- Battery/crank power
- First aid kit
- 72-hour supplies (water, food, meds)

**3. Seal Capability (Chemical/Biological):**
- Plastic sheeting (10x10 ft per window): $20
- Duct tape: 3-4 rolls
- Cut plastic to fit windows/doors in advance
- Practice sealing (can do in <10 minutes)

**4. Temperature Control:**
- **Heat:** Fireplace, wood stove, propane heater (Mr. Heater Buddy)
- **Cool:** Battery fans, wet towels, basement retreat
- **Insulation:** Heavy curtains, draft stoppers

**5. Lighting:**
- LED lanterns (battery): 2-3
- Headlamps: 1 per person
- Candles: 20-50 (fire risk - careful)
- Glow sticks: 20-30 (safe for kids)
- Extra batteries

**6. Communications:**
- Battery/crank radio (weather alerts)
- Charged phone + power banks
- Paper map of area
- Emergency contact list (printed)

### Emergency Toilet (Bucket System)

**When Plumbing Fails:**

**Supplies:**
- 5-gallon bucket with lid
- Heavy-duty trash bags (13-30 gallon)
- Pool noodle or toilet seat (fits bucket)
- Kitty litter, sawdust, or peat moss
- Toilet paper
- Hand sanitizer

**Setup:**
1. Line bucket with trash bag
2. Add 2-3 inches kitty litter/sawdust
3. Cut pool noodle to fit rim (cushion), or use portable toilet seat
4. Place in private area

**Use:**
1. Do business as normal
2. Add scoop of litter/sawdust after each use (covers smell, absorbs)
3. Wipe with TP (put in bucket, not separate)
4. Hand sanitizer

**Disposal:**
- Change bag when 1/2-2/3 full
- Tie tightly
- Store outside in trash can (lid secure)
- Municipal pickup when restored, or bury (see below)

**Smell Control:**
- Use litter/sawdust generously
- Keep lid on
- Change bag frequently
- Baking soda sprinkle helps

**Urine Separation (Advanced):**
- Pee in separate container (milk jug)
- Dilute 10:1 with water, pour on garden/grass (fertilizer)
- Reduces bucket fill rate, smell

**Cost:** $20-30 total

### Heating Without Power

**Dangers:** Carbon monoxide (CO) poisoning kills many in disasters

**NEVER Use Indoors:**
- Charcoal grill (high CO)
- Propane camp stove (prolonged use)
- Gas-powered generator (must be 20+ ft from home)
- Gasoline heater

**Safe Indoor Options:**

**1. Fireplace (if you have one):**
- Stock firewood: 1/2-1 cord (short-term)
- Keep chimney swept (prevents fire)
- Fireplace screen (prevent embers)
- Ash bucket

**2. Wood Stove:**
- More efficient than fireplace
- Heats larger area
- Can cook on top
- Requires chimney/vent installation

**3. Propane Heater (Mr. Heater Buddy):**
- Indoor-safe with oxygen depletion sensor
- Uses 1 lb propane canisters (9 hrs per canister on low)
- Heats 200-300 sq ft
- Cost: $80-120
- Propane: $4-6 per canister (stock 20-50)
- **Still need ventilation** (crack window)

**4. Kerosene Heater (Indoor-rated):**
- 10,000-23,000 BTU
- Burns 8-12 hours per gallon
- Ventilation required
- Fuel storage concerns
- Cost: $150-300

**5. Passive Heating:**
- Close off rooms (heat only 1-2 rooms)
- Hang blankets over doorways (insulate)
- Everyone in same room (body heat)
- Dress in layers (see below)
- Hot water bottles in sleeping bags

**Carbon Monoxide Detector:**
- Battery-powered required
- Check batteries monthly
- $20-40
- **ESSENTIAL if using any combustion heat**

### Clothing Layers for Warmth

**Instead of Heating Entire Home:**

**Base Layer (Next to Skin):**
- Synthetic (polyester) or wool
- NOT cotton (stays wet, makes you cold)
- Long underwear top and bottom

**Middle Layer (Insulation):**
- Fleece jacket or wool sweater
- Puffy jacket (down or synthetic)

**Outer Layer (Wind/Water Protection):**
- Windbreaker or rain jacket
- Keeps warmth in, elements out

**Extremities (Lose Heat Fast):**
- Hat (lose 40% heat from head - myth, but head/neck important)
- Gloves or mittens (mittens warmer)
- Warm socks (wool or synthetic, 2 pairs)
- Insulated boots

**Sleeping:**
- Sleep in base layers + hat
- Use sleeping bag (rated for temp)
- Emergency blanket (mylar) under sleeping bag (reflects heat)
- Hot water bottle at feet
- Multiple people share warmth (sleep close)

### Cooling Without Power

**Hot Weather Shelter:**

**Passive Cooling:**
1. **Block sun:** Close blinds/curtains on sunny side
2. **Retreat to coolest area:** Basement (underground = cooler), north side of home
3. **Create airflow:** Open windows at night, close during day (if outside cooler than inside)
4. **Cross-ventilation:** Windows on opposite sides
5. **Wet towels:** Hang in windows (evaporative cooling)
6. **Spray bottle + fan:** Mist skin, fan evaporates (cools)

**Hydration:**
- Drink 1-2 gallons per day (hot weather)
- Electrolytes (see TOPIC_WATER.md)
- Avoid alcohol, caffeine (dehydrate)

**Clothing:**
- Light colors (reflect sun)
- Loose, breathable (cotton OK for heat)
- Wide-brimmed hat
- Wet bandana around neck

**Battery Fans:**
- USB fans + power bank
- 10" battery fan (20-40 hours on batteries): $30-50
- Stock extra batteries

**Avoid Heat Illness:**
- Stay in shade
- Reduce activity during hottest hours (10am-4pm)
- Watch for signs (see MEDICAL_FIRST_AID.md)

## Evacuation Scenarios

### When to Evacuate

**Mandatory Evacuation Orders:**
- Hurricane (coastal)
- Wildfire
- Flood
- Chemical spill
- Dam failure

**Self-Evacuation (Before Mandatory):**
- Safer than waiting
- Less traffic
- More options

### Evacuation Destinations

**Priority Order:**

**1. Friend/Family (50-100+ miles away):**
- Free
- Familiar, comfortable
- Arrange in advance

**2. Hotel/Motel:**
- Book early (fills fast)
- Costs $80-200+ per night
- Bring supplies (may not be able to buy)

**3. Official Evacuation Shelter:**
- School, community center, church
- Free
- Crowded, noisy, no privacy
- Cots, basic supplies
- Red Cross often operates

**4. Campground:**
- If you have RV/trailer/tent
- $20-50 per night
- Access to facilities

**5. Rest Stop / Parking Lot (Last Resort):**
- Overnight in vehicle
- Free but uncomfortable
- Safety concerns

### Bug-Out Bag (72-Hour Evacuation Pack)

**One Per Person, Ready to Grab**

**Container:**
- Backpack (40-60L adults, 20-30L kids)
- Hands-free for travel
- Durable, waterproof or with rain cover

**Clothing:**
- 3 sets underwear, socks
- 2 shirts, 2 pants
- 1 jacket/sweater
- Rain gear
- Hat, gloves (cold climate)
- Sturdy shoes (worn, extras in bag)

**Shelter:**
- Emergency blanket (mylar): 2
- Tarp (8x10 ft): 1
- Paracord (50-100 ft)
- Tent (optional, if room/weight allows)

**Water:**
- 2-3 liters in bottles
- Water filter (Sawyer Mini, LifeStraw)
- Purification tablets (backup)

**Food:**
- 3 days, 2,000+ calories/day
- Energy bars, trail mix, jerky
- Lightweight, no cooking required
- See TOPIC_FOOD.md

**Fire:**
- Lighter: 2-3
- Waterproof matches
- Ferro rod (backup)
- Tinder (dryer lint, petroleum jelly cotton balls)

**First Aid:**
- See MEDICAL_FIRST_AID.md kit
- Personal medications (2-week supply)

**Tools:**
- Knife (fixed blade or quality folder)
- Multi-tool
- Duct tape (wrap around water bottle)
- Safety pins
- Paracord

**Light:**
- Headlamp + extra batteries
- Flashlight
- Glow sticks

**Documents (Waterproof Bag):**
- ID copies (license, passport)
- Insurance papers (home, auto, medical)
- Bank account info
- Property deed/lease
- Medical records
- Emergency contacts
- Cash ($200-500, small bills)
- USB drive (scanned documents)

**Communication:**
- Phone charger + power bank
- Battery/crank radio
- Whistle (signal for help)
- Paper map of region

**Hygiene:**
- Toothbrush, toothpaste (travel size)
- Soap, shampoo (travel size)
- Toilet paper (1 roll, remove cardboard, compress)
- Hand sanitizer
- Feminine products
- Ziplock bags (waste, waterproofing)

**Personal:**
- Glasses (spare pair)
- Contact lenses + solution
- Prescription meds
- Comfort item for kids (small toy, book)
- Notebook, pencil

**Total Weight:**
- Adults: 20-40 lbs max (1/4 body weight)
- Kids: 10-20 lbs (lighter)
- Elderly: Reduce weight (prioritize essentials)

**Storage:**
- Near main exit
- Everyone knows location
- Check/rotate every 6-12 months

## Emergency Shelter Construction

**If Forced to Build Shelter (Wilderness, Homeless, Disaster)**

### Site Selection

**Priorities:**
1. **Flat ground** (comfort, water won't pool)
2. **Drainage** (avoid low spots, gullies)
3. **Protection from wind** (trees, rock walls, hills)
4. **Water access** (100+ ft away to avoid flooding/contamination, but close enough to access)
5. **Avoid hazards:**
   - Dead trees (widowmakers - fall in wind)
   - Flash flood areas (dry creek beds)
   - Avalanche/rockfall zones
   - Insect nests
6. **Sun exposure** (balance: morning sun good, afternoon shade in summer)

---

### Tarp Shelter (Fastest, Most Versatile)

**Tarp Sizes:**
- 8x10 ft: 1-2 people
- 10x12 ft: 2-3 people
- 12x16 ft: 4+ people

**Cost:** $15-50 (quality matters - reinforced grommets)

**Setup #1: A-Frame (Classic)**
1. Tie paracord between 2 trees (6-8 ft high)
2. Drape tarp over cord (ridge line)
3. Stake out corners (45-degree angle)
4. Optional: Fold sides under for floor

**Setup #2: Lean-To (Simple)**
1. Tie one edge of tarp high (6-7 ft) between trees
2. Stake opposite edge low (ground or 1-2 ft)
3. Stake sides
4. Open on one side (face away from wind)

**Setup #3: Plow Point (Wind/Rain)**
1. Stake one corner to ground
2. Opposite corner tied high to tree
3. Stake other two corners out (forms triangle)
4. Wind/rain hits point, diverts

**Setup #4: Flying Diamond (Full Coverage)**
1. Stake center of tarp high (pole or tree)
2. Stake all 4 corners out (pyramid shape)
3. Good for sun/rain, poor for wind

**Tarp Tips:**
- Use paracord (550 cord: Strong, lightweight)
- Taut-line hitch (adjustable knot)
- Pad stakes (prevents tearing grommets)
- Collect water in sag (rain collection)

---

### Natural Shelter (No Tarp)

**Debris Hut (Insulated, Warm):**

**How to Build:**
1. **Frame:** Long ridge pole (8-10 ft) propped at 45 degrees on tree/fork
2. **Ribs:** Sticks laid against ridge pole (ribcage shape, both sides)
3. **Debris:** Pile leaves, pine needles, grass 2-3 ft thick (insulation)
4. **Door:** Stuff opening with leaves/pack
5. **Interior:** More leaves inside (bedding)

**Pros:** Warm, windproof, insulated
**Cons:** Time-consuming (2-4 hours), gets dirty
**Use:** Cold weather, multi-day stay

---

**Lean-To (Quick, Simple):**

**How to Build:**
1. **Ridge pole:** Horizontal pole between 2 trees (4-5 ft high)
2. **Rafters:** Lean long sticks against ridge pole (angle)
3. **Thatching:** Weave/layer branches, leaves, bark across rafters (shingle from bottom up)
4. **Bedding:** Thick layer of leaves/pine needles

**Pros:** Fast (1-2 hours), simple
**Cons:** Open on one side, poor in wind
**Use:** Warm weather, overnight

---

**Snow Cave/Quinzhee (Winter):**

**Snow Cave (if snowdrift available):**
1. Dig entrance tunnel into drift (upward angle)
2. Hollow out chamber (dome shape, 5-6 ft wide)
3. Smooth ceiling (prevents dripping)
4. Poke ventilation hole (ski pole, stick) in roof
5. Block entrance partially (snow block, pack)

**Quinzhee (build snow pile):**
1. Pile snow in dome (8-10 ft wide, 5-6 ft tall)
2. Let consolidate 2-3 hours
3. Dig entrance tunnel
4. Hollow out (1 ft thick walls - use stick markers)
5. Ventilation hole in roof

**Warmth:** Insulated, reflects body heat (can be 30-40°F warmer inside)
**Danger:** CO buildup (MUST have ventilation hole), collapse (make walls strong)

**Cost:** Free (labor intensive)
**Time:** 3-6 hours

## Vehicle as Shelter

**Car/Truck as Emergency Shelter:**

### Advantages
- Weatherproof
- Lockable (security)
- Can run heat (limited)
- Mobile (can relocate)

### Preparation

**Keep in Vehicle Year-Round:**
- Blankets or sleeping bags: 1 per seat
- Water: 1 gallon per person
- Food: Granola bars, nuts (replace every 6 months)
- First aid kit
- Flashlight, batteries
- Jumper cables
- Ice scraper, shovel (winter)
- Sand/cat litter (traction in snow)
- Duct tape, paracord
- Portable phone charger

### Sleeping in Vehicle

**Warmth:**
- Sleeping bag + blankets
- Dress in layers
- Hat and gloves (critical)
- Emergency blanket (mylar) over sleeping bag
- Cardboard under sleeping bag (insulation from cold seat)

**Ventilation:**
- Crack windows 1-2 inches (CO and condensation)
- **NEVER run car continuously** (CO poisoning)
- Run 10-15 min per hour to warm up, then turn off

**Privacy:**
- Curtains or sunshades in windows
- Tinted windows help
- Park inconspicuously

**Safety:**
- Lock doors
- Keys accessible (quick escape)
- Phone charged
- Park in well-lit area (if possible) or hidden (depends on threat)

**Condensation:**
- Breathing creates moisture (will freeze on windows in cold)
- Crack windows
- Wipe down windows in morning
- Desiccant packs help

## Weather-Specific Considerations

### Cold Weather Shelter

**Priorities:**
1. Insulation from ground (sleeping pad, cardboard, pine boughs)
2. Block wind
3. Retain body heat (small space, seal drafts)
4. Stay dry (wet = hypothermia)

**Shelter Types:**
- Enclosed (4 walls, roof)
- Small (body heat warms faster)
- Insulated (debris, snow)

**Bedding:**
- Thick insulation from ground (lose heat to ground faster than air)
- Layer sleeping bags if cold

---

### Hot Weather Shelter

**Priorities:**
1. Shade (sun protection)
2. Airflow (ventilation)
3. Elevation (off ground if possible)
4. Water access

**Shelter Types:**
- Open sides (cross-breeze)
- Light-colored materials (reflect heat)
- Large (allows hot air to rise)

**Avoid:**
- Enclosed shelters (trap heat)
- Dark materials (absorb heat)
- Low areas (hot air sinks)

---

### Wet Weather Shelter

**Priorities:**
1. Waterproof roof (tarp, thick thatching)
2. Drainage (slope ground away, dig trench around shelter)
3. Elevated floor (off wet ground)
4. Seal gaps (wind-driven rain)

**Tarp Tips:**
- Steep angle (45+ degrees) sheds rain
- Taut (no sagging pools)
- Extend beyond walls (drip line)

**Natural Shelter:**
- Thick thatching (shingle from bottom up, overlap)
- 2-3 ft thick debris (waterproofs)

## First 24 Hours Priorities

**Immediate Actions After Disaster:**

1. **Assess safety:** Structure damage, gas leaks, fires
2. **Account for people:** Everyone safe, injured?
3. **Secure shelter:** Board broken windows, tarp roof damage, close off unsafe areas
4. **Establish sanitation:** Toilet system if plumbing out
5. **Inventory supplies:** Water, food, meds (what you have, what you need)
6. **Communicate:** Contact family, authorities (if phones work)
7. **Information:** Radio for updates, instructions
8. **Security:** Lock doors if civil unrest, board windows
9. **Rest:** Adrenaline crash - take shifts if needed
10. **Plan next 24 hours:** Ration supplies, assign tasks

## Days 2-7: Stabilization

**Goals:**
- Establish routine
- Conserve resources
- Maintain hygiene
- Gather information
- Prepare for longer duration

**Daily Routine:**
- Wake/sleep regular times (maintain normalcy)
- Meals at set times (ration)
- Hygiene (wash hands, brush teeth)
- Radio check (news updates)
- Inventory check (track supplies)
- Security patrol (check perimeter, damage)
- Entertainment (books, games, conversation - morale)
- Exercise (maintain health, prevent cabin fever)

**Improvements:**
- Insulate shelter better (blankets on windows)
- Organize supplies (easy access)
- Set up charging station (solar, car)
- Establish better sanitation (privacy, odor control)
- Expand water collection (tarps, buckets)

## Week 2: Transition

**By Week 2:**
- Emergency likely subsiding OR long-term scenario clear
- Supplies running low
- Routines established
- Morale challenges

**Assess:**
- **End in sight?** Power/water restoring, evacuations lifting → hold out
- **Long-term?** No services, government collapse → transition to long-term plans (see SHELTER_CONSTRUCTION.md)

**Resupply:**
- If stores opening: Restock immediately
- Barter/trade with neighbors
- Mutual aid groups

**Mental Health:**
- Stress, anxiety normal
- Talk through experience
- Kids need routine, play, normalcy
- Recognize signs of crisis (see MEDICAL_WILDERNESS.md)
